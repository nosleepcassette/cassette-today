**Little Exceeding (#62)**
*thunder, mountain, the arousing*

The image of Little Exceeding describes thunder on the mountain — a powerful force contained, or at least, attempting to be contained. This is an instruction to balance the desire to act, to move, to create, with the need to respect the boundaries and the timing of the situation. The mountain represents stillness, and the thunder represents the energy that wants to break through that stillness. The image suggests that we must find a way to express our energy, our creativity, and our passions without overwhelming the situation or forcing our way through.

The judgment of Little Exceeding says: "_PREPONDERANCE OF THE SMALL. Success. Perseverance furthers. Small things may be done; great things should not be done._" This judgment is about the importance of humility and moderation in our actions. It's not saying that we should never aim high or strive for great things, but rather that we should focus on the small, manageable steps that lead to success, rather than trying to force our way to greatness. The emphasis is on perseverance and careful, consistent action, rather than grand gestures or sudden, dramatic changes.

Little Exceeding names a time when carefulness and attention to detail are crucial. It demands a posture of humility and a willingness to listen and observe, rather than to act impulsively or aggressively. This hexagram sees that the situation is delicate, and that the wrong move could have significant consequences. It advises us to be cautious, to think before we act, and to prioritize thrift and simplicity in our expenditures and our actions. The superior man, in this situation, gives preponderance to reverence, to grief, and to thrift, recognizing that these qualities will ultimately lead to success and good fortune.

## synthesis
The single card, The Moon, set the morning's tone with its dreamy, intuitive energy. The three-card spread showed a psychological arc from The Moon, to the Six of Cups, to the Six of Wands (reversed), suggesting a movement from confusion and uncertainty, to a desire for comfort and familiarity, to a fear of success or recognition. The hexagram, Little Exceeding, shows the structural layer underneath both, advising caution, humility, and perseverance. The tarot and the I Ching see the same thing from different angles — the need for carefulness, the importance of moderation, and the danger of forcing our way or striving too hard for greatness. The I Ching complicates the tarot by adding a layer of depth and nuance, highlighting the importance of respecting the timing and the boundaries of the situation. The tarot, in turn, adds a layer of psychological insight, showing how our fears and desires may be driving our actions. Practically, this means that we should approach the day with caution and humility, prioritizing small, manageable steps and careful, consistent action. We should be willing to listen and observe, rather than acting impulsively or aggressively. And we should recognize that success and good fortune will come from perseverance and carefulness, rather than from grand gestures or sudden, dramatic changes.
