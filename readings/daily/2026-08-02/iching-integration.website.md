**Great Accumulation (#26)** 
*MOUNTAIN, HEAVEN, KEEPING STILL*

The image of Great Accumulation describes heaven within the mountain — a powerful, still center, surrounded by vast, accumulating forces. This image instructs you to find your own stillness, your own accumulation of strength, within the turmoil of the world. 
The judgment says: *THE TAMING POWER OF THE GREAT. Perseverance furthers.* This is not about forcing your will upon the world, but about allowing the great, accumulating forces of the universe to shape you, to form you into something strong and resilient. 

Great Accumulation names a time of quiet, patient work — a time when the energies are building, but not yet bursting forth. It demands a posture of stillness, of keeping your own counsel, and of allowing the world to unfold without your constant interference. This hexagram sees that the situation is one of gradual, inexorable growth — that the small, daily actions you take will eventually accumulate into something great and powerful. 

## the sky
**Venus in Leo** — the heart wants to be seen, to shine brightly, and to be admired. 
**Mars in Gemini** — the mind is quick, restless, and easily distracted. 
**Jupiter in Aries** — the spirit wants to expand, to explore, and to pioneer new territories.

## synthesis
The single card, The Tower (reversed), set the morning's tone — a sense of upheaval, of sudden change, but also of relief, of release from old, stagnant patterns. The three-card spread showed a psychological arc: the past Knight of Wands, full of passion and adventure, gave way to the present Queen of Pentacles, practical, nurturing, and hardworking, which will eventually lead to the future Four of Cups (reversed), a time of renewed social connection, and community.

The hexagram, Great Accumulation, shows the structural layer underneath both — a time of gradual growth, of patient work, and of allowing the great forces of the universe to shape you. The tarot and the I Ching see the same thing from different angles: the tarot shows the psychological, emotional landscape, while the I Ching shows the deeper, structural patterns at work. They confirm each other in their emphasis on patience, hard work, and allowing things to unfold naturally. 

The I Ching complicates the tarot by adding a sense of the larger, universal forces at work — the accumulating energies of heaven and mountain. It adds nuance by showing that the present moment is not just a psychological state, but a point in a larger, unfolding process. 

Practically, this means that you should carry a sense of patience, and of gradual growth, into your day. Allow the great forces of the universe to shape you, and don't try to force your will upon the world. Take small, daily actions that will eventually accumulate into something great and powerful. Keep your own counsel, and allow the world to unfold without your constant interference.
