**Mutual Influence (#31)**  
*Lake over Mountain — The Joyous over Keeping Still*  
*mutual influence, receptivity, attraction, approach*

A lake rests on the mountain. Literally, this is an impossible little arrangement — water held high above the ground, pleasure gathered over stillness, feeling suspended over something that does not move. The image describes influence as something that happens through presence, not force. The mountain does not chase the lake. The lake does not conquer the mountain. Each changes the atmosphere of the other simply by being there.

The instruction is to become receptive enough that people, information, and possibility can approach you. Not passive. Not porous to every demand. Receptive. There is a difference. The superior person encourages others to come near by being ready to receive them, not by performing need, issuing commands, or trying to control the outcome before anything has had time to become real.

The judgment says: *“Influence. Success. Perseverance furthers. To take a maiden to wife brings good fortune.”* This is not only about romance, although the image of courtship is deliberate. It describes the beginning of a bond — the moment when attraction becomes a structure, when feeling has to prove it can survive contact with time. Influence succeeds when it is steady, mutual, and allowed to develop in the right order. You do not get to skip the part where trust becomes familiar.

Hexagram 31 names a time when you are affecting one another, whether anyone is admitting it yet or not. The question is not whether influence exists. It does. The question is what kind you are participating in. Are you inviting approach, or trying to pull someone toward you? Are you listening for a real response, or interpreting every flicker as confirmation? Are you letting the other person remain a person, with their own pace, limits, and interior weather?

This hexagram asks for a posture that can receive without grasping. It sees attraction as a field between bodies, minds, and choices — not a prize awarded to the most intense participant. Mutual influence has no use for a one-person monologue disguised as chemistry.

Line 5 says: *“The influence shows itself in the back of the neck.”* The neck turns the head, but it does not initiate the movement. This is influence at the level of instinct, sensitivity, and involuntary response. Something catches your attention before your rational mind has decided what it means. Today, this line warns you not to confuse a bodily reaction with a complete instruction. A pull is real. It is still only a pull.

The back of the neck also carries tension. You may notice yourself bracing, looking over your shoulder, waiting for the next sign, the next message, the next shift in someone’s tone. That vigilance can make influence feel like danger. The gift here is learning to register what moves through you without immediately turning it into an action. Let the body tell you something. Do not make it write the whole story.

Line 6 says: *“The influence shows itself in the jaws, cheeks, and tongue.”* Here influence reaches speech — the face, the mouth, the words that make a private feeling public. This is the line of saying too much, saying too soon, or allowing the mouth to turn uncertainty into a declaration. It can also be the gift of honest expression, but only when speech follows perception instead of trying to manufacture it.

Together, Lines 5 and 6 build a clear movement: first the body reacts, then the mouth wants to explain. You feel the shift in your nervous system, and then you reach for language to secure it. That sequence is where the day can go wrong. The neck notices. The tongue announces. The oracle says: leave a little space between those events.

Today, notice what your body is responding to before you decide what it means. If you need to speak, speak plainly, and stop before the explanation becomes a campaign. Influence grows through reciprocal movement. It gets strangled by premature certainty.

### relating hexagram: 56 — the Wanderer (旅)

When the changing lines complete their movement, Mutual Influence becomes **The Wanderer (#56), Travelling (旅)**. The Chinese name, *Lü*, describes the stranger, the traveler, the person temporarily outside their established home and social position. The judgment says: *“The Wanderer. Success through smallness. Perseverance brings good fortune to the wanderer.”* A wanderer succeeds by remembering that they are passing through.

This is where the day is going. Mutual Influence begins with attraction and receptivity — two forces sensing one another across a distance. The Wanderer asks what happens after the first contact, when nobody gets to assume ownership. You are moving through someone else’s world, or they are moving through yours. You need tact. You need proportion. You need to know what belongs to you, and what does not.

The relating hexagram does not cancel the influence. It gives it a condition: keep it small enough to remain true. Do not demand permanence from a moment that is still introducing itself. Do not become gruff or overbearing because you are uncertain of your place. A traveler who behaves like the landlord gets shown the door. Fair enough.

## the sky

**Sun in Sagittarius (12th house)** — your sense of aliveness is wandering through private territory, looking for meaning beneath the obvious story rather than performing certainty in public.

**Moon in Gemini (6th house)** — your feelings want data, conversation, and something useful to do with all that nervous energy.

**Mercury in Scorpio (11th house)** — your mind is reading beneath the surface of friendships, futures, and group dynamics, looking for what people are not saying.

The sky adds a strange but accurate complication. The Sun is in Sagittarius, but tucked into the twelfth house — expansive fire behind a curtain. You may want freedom, novelty, and a larger meaning, while also needing solitude from the personality you keep presenting to everyone else. The Moon in Gemini wants to process through details, messages, errands, and small acts of usefulness. Mercury in Scorpio is less innocent about communication. It hears the pause in the sentence. It notices who viewed the story. It remembers the exact word choice from three weeks ago, because apparently the brain has decided to become a forensic lab.

That combination makes receptivity possible, but it also makes overinterpretation very tempting. The Moon wants information. Mercury wants the hidden motive. The Sun wants an escape hatch into a bigger life. None of those impulses are wrong. Together, they can turn a simple interaction into an archaeological dig.

You are not being asked to prove your devotion by hauling every emotional possibility up the mountain yourself. Put something down. Especially the part where you assume that if you stop pushing, everything will disappear.

The tarot and the I Ching are looking at the same moment from different depths. The Ten of Wands reversed names the burden you are releasing. The Lovers names the live wire of mutual choice. The King of Cups shows the emotional posture that can hold the connection without turning it into a crisis. Hexagram 31 describes the structure underneath: influence is real, but it only becomes beneficial when both people remain free to approach, respond, pause, and change their minds.

The changing lines complicate The Lovers by bringing the body and the mouth into the room. You may feel something strongly. You may understand something sharply. You may want to say the exact sentence that would make the whole situation resolve itself. Do not make your tongue carry the weight you just set down.

Let the invitation be an invitation. Let the answer arrive as an answer. Today, your work is not to force closeness or retreat into indifference. It is to stay present, say what is true in a size the moment can hold, and leave enough room for the other person to move toward you under their own power.
