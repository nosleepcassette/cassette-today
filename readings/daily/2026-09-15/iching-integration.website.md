**Little Exceeding (#62)**
*thunder, mountain, the arousing, small things, exceeding, carefulness, humility*

imagine thunder rumbling across a mountain peak— a huge, powerful force moving over something ancient and unyielding. that's the image for hexagram 62, little exceeding. it's not thunder *under* the mountain, which would be contained, but *on* it. it suggests a slight imbalance, where something small or seemingly insignificant has come to have undue weight, or where something large is trying to force its way into a space too small for it. the superior person, faced with this, gives their focus to reverence, to grief, to thrift. you're meant to mind the small things, the quiet duties, the internal posture.

the judgment is clear: *preponderance of the small. success. perseverance furthers. small things may be done; great things should not be done.* this isn't about avoiding ambition entirely, it's about discerning the right scale for right now. you're in a time where the micro matters more than the macro. big, sweeping gestures are likely to fail, or worse, to backfire. your success hinges on careful, consistent attention to the details, to the foundations, to the humble work that often goes unnoticed. it's a moment for quiet strength, not for grand pronouncements.

this hexagram names a time when the ordinary order is subtly inverted, or when things are slightly out of proportion. the small is exceeding its proper measure— perhaps it's your own cautious nature, or a minor detail that's taken on outsized importance, or even a small project that's demanding more energy than you anticipated. this calls for exceptional modesty and conscientiousness, a deep respect for what is, rather than what you wish it to be. you can make progress, absolutely, but you do it by holding dignity in your personal behavior, by not letting your carefulness become subservience, and by understanding the true demands of the current moment. it asks you to lean into the quiet power of restraint, to build rather than to conquer.

the first line of the hexagram warns you: *the bird meets with misfortune through flying.* this is a direct instruction against overreach, especially now. if you try to take flight, to expand too quickly, or to aim for something far beyond your current capabilities, you'll find yourself in trouble. this isn't the time to test your wings in unfamiliar skies. stay low. stay grounded. trust the earth beneath your feet.

further into this moment, the fourth line echoes the caution: *no blame. he meets him without passing by. going brings danger. one must be on guard. do not act. be constantly persevering.* you might encounter an opportunity, a person, or a situation that feels like a call to action. the impulse will be there to engage, to push forward. but the wisdom here is to hold back. to remain in place. to meet what comes without rushing past it, without trying to force a resolution. it's a call for vigilance, for observation, for a steady, unwavering commitment to your current path without outward movement. you're not meant to advance; you're meant to hold your ground with unwavering presence.

this is where you're headed if you don't heed the call to carefulness. hexagram 62, little exceeding, moves toward **hexagram 36: Brilliance Injured (明夷)**. its judgment warns of the *darkening of the light. in adversity it furthers one to be persevering.* this is not a destination you want to rush into. if you push too hard, if you ignore the calls for modesty and restraint, if you try to make great things happen when only small things are appropriate, you risk obscuring your own light. your brilliance, your true capabilities, might be hidden or even damaged. the arc here is clear: respect the small, stay grounded, and protect your inner strength by not overextending. endure, yes, but do so by being smart about how and when you reveal yourself.
