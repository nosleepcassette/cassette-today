## the card

**Queen of Swords (upright) — focus**

*discernment, independence, honesty, boundaries*

The Queen of Swords sees clearly and says what she sees. Her discernment has been sharpened by experience, not detached from it. She knows that a boundary can be compassionate and firm at the same time, and that understanding someone's reasons does not require accepting every consequence of their behavior.

Start with the distinction between a fact and the story built around it. What was actually said? What agreement was made, and what happened afterward? What do you need now? These questions can bring a tangled conversation down to a size you can work with. They also leave room for the possibility that you don't have all the information yet.

The Queen is not an invitation to use precision as a weapon. There is a particular kind of sentence that is technically accurate and designed to draw blood. You can choose another one. Clear language protects the truth without requiring you to make a spectacle of how sharply you can see it.

## a clean boundary

Pick one situation where you have been explaining yourself in circles. Write the request or limit in a single sentence: “I can do this part, but I can't take on the rest,” or “I need an answer by Friday to make my plans.” Adjust the words to fit the actual relationship. You can listen to a response without surrendering the boundary to a vote.

Independence does not mean never needing anyone. It means keeping your own judgment in the room when other people have strong opinions. Ask for advice if it helps; check facts before you decide; notice when fear is filling a gap in the evidence. You are allowed to revise your view when you learn something new.

There is compassion in making things plain. It gives another person a chance to understand what you mean, and it gives you relief from defending a limit in ten different ways. Say the thing you can stand behind. Then let the silence do some of the work.
