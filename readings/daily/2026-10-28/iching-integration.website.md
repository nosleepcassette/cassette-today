**Contention (#6)**
heaven, water, the creative

heaven and water go their opposite ways — a literal picture of conflict. it’s not just two things disagreeing, it’s two fundamental forces moving in inherently opposite directions. one rises, one falls. this hexagram opens with that image, telling you that the situation before you is one of fundamental divergence. it asks you to consider the very beginning of things, to understand the root of this opposing motion before you take another step.

the judgment is direct, it doesn't pull punches:
*"CONFLICT. You are sincere / And are being obstructed. / A cautious halt halfway brings good fortune. / Going through to the end brings misfortune. / It furthers one to see the great man. / It does not further one to cross the great water."*

you’re not wrong here, not fundamentally. your intentions are sincere, but something is in the way. the crucial instruction is to stop. don't push through to the end, because that path leads to misfortune. it's not about winning, it's about avoiding disaster. when faced with this kind of inherent opposition, you need to seek counsel, to find someone with wisdom and impartiality — "the great man." and you absolutely do not "cross the great water," which means you don't escalate, you don't embark on a risky, all-or-nothing venture. this is a time for strategic retreat, for careful assessment, for understanding that sometimes, the best move is no move at all.

hexagram 6 names a moment when genuine intent meets immovable opposition. it’s a time when you might feel righteous, but the path forward is blocked, or the cost of pushing through is too high. the posture it demands is one of self-awareness and strategic wisdom — you’re not being asked to surrender your truth, but to understand the terrain of the battle. it sees the situation as inherently fraught, requiring a different kind of strength than brute force. it’s not about who is 'right,' it’s about what outcome serves your highest good, and often, that’s not found at the end of a fight.

the fourth line speaks to a deep wisdom:
*"One cannot engage in conflict. / One turns back and submits to fate, / Changes one's attitude, / And finds peace in perseverance. / Good fortune."*
this isn't about giving up, it's about recognizing when a conflict is unwinnable, or simply not worth the energy. it’s a powerful act of self-preservation to turn away, to shift your perspective, and to find your peace not in victory, but in accepting what is, and then persevering through that acceptance. this is about freeing yourself from the need to fight.

the fifth line offers a specific way out, should you need it:
*"To contend before him / Brings supreme good fortune."*
if you absolutely must contend, do it before an impartial authority. this is not about a personal squabble, but about seeking justice through proper, fair channels. it’s about bringing your truth to someone who can truly arbitrate, not engaging directly with the source of the obstruction. this line is a beacon for clarity and fair process when conflict cannot be entirely avoided.

the sixth line, the very top of the hexagram, warns against the hollow triumph:
*"Even if by chance a leather belt is bestowed on one,' / By the end of a morning / It will have been snatched away three times."*
this is the ultimate caution against "winning" the conflict. even if you gain something, it’s fleeting, unstable, and will be taken from you again and again. it's a stark reminder that some victories are not worth having, because they don't bring lasting peace or security. the true prize lies in understanding the futility of such struggles.

this journey through contention, with its warnings and guidance, resolves into hexagram 7, Multitude (師). from individual struggles and strategic withdrawals, you are moving towards a collective, organized force. the "great man" you were advised to see in hexagram 6's judgment becomes the "strong man" needed here to lead and organize the "mass" of the army. this isn't about waging war, but about discipline, shared purpose, and the strength found in order. the contention, once understood and managed, can be channeled into cohesive action. it’s the landing point where individual struggles find purpose within a larger, disciplined structure.

## synthesis
