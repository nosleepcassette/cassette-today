**Remedying (#18)**
MOUNTAIN above WIND
*remedying, keeping still, decay, stirring up*

the wind blows low on the mountain — this is the image of decay, of something that has slowly, quietly eroded over time. it’s not a sudden storm or a dramatic collapse, but the persistent, subtle force that wears things down at their base. it’s what happens when you don’t notice, or when you choose not to act. the instruction here is clear: the superior person sees this, and rather than letting the decay continue, they stir up the people, they strengthen the spirit. it’s an active call to renewal, a rallying cry against stagnation. you are being asked to become that superior person.

the judgment is direct, almost bracing: *“work on what has been spoiled. has supreme success. it furthers one to cross the great water. before the starting point, three days. after the starting point, three days.”* this isn't a suggestion, it's a mandate. something is broken, something has decayed, and it’s your job to fix it. but this isn’t just grunt work; it promises "supreme success." it’s a big undertaking, a journey across "the great water," which means you’re tackling something significant, something that might feel overwhelming. but you’re given a buffer: time to prepare, time to integrate. three days before you even begin, three days to let it settle afterward. this isn't a rush job. it’s a deliberate, thoughtful, and ultimately deeply rewarding process of restoration.

this hexagram names a very particular kind of time — a time when the consequences of past neglect, or even just benign oversight, become undeniable. it’s not about finding fault, though. it’s about recognizing that the current state of things is untenable, and that action is required. the posture it demands is one of courageous responsibility. you’re not just observing the rot; you’re engaging with it, rolling up your sleeves to clean it out. it’s about understanding that the integrity of the whole depends on addressing these underlying issues, and that doing so isn't just for your own benefit, but for the health of whatever system you’re a part of. it’s a deep clean, a necessary reset.

the first line speaks to **Line 1: setting right what has been spoiled by the father. if there is a son, no blame rests upon the departed father. danger. in the end good fortune.** this is about inherited issues, the foundational problems that weren’t yours to create but are absolutely yours to face. it might be a family pattern, an organizational culture, or even just old habits that were passed down. the "father" here represents the past, the origin point. you’re picking up the pieces, mending the foundational cracks. there’s a sense of danger in confronting these deep-seated issues — it takes courage to look at what's been left unresolved. but the hexagram assures you, the outcome is "good fortune." you are breaking a cycle, and that's a powerful act.

moving deeper, **Line 3: setting right what has been spoiled by the father. there will be a little remorse. no great blame.** you’re still working on those inherited issues, but here, the emotional landscape is a bit softer. you might feel a pang of sadness or regret for how things got this way, a touch of melancholy for the lost potential, or the sheer effort required. but it’s not paralyzing guilt or overwhelming anger. it’s a necessary emotion in the process of healing, a quiet acknowledgment of the past. it allows you to continue the work without getting stuck in blame. it’s a quiet ache, a necessary part of the process.

the work you’re doing starts to become visible now, with **Line 5: setting right what has been spoiled by the father. one meets with praise.** your efforts are not going unnoticed. the difficult, often thankless task of remediation is being recognized and appreciated. this isn't about seeking external validation, but about the natural consequence of doing good, necessary work. you’re not just cleaning up a mess; you’re setting a new standard, creating something better, and that positive impact resonates. it’s a moment of collective acknowledgment for your diligence and commitment.

finally, **Line 6: he does not serve kings and princes, sets himself higher goals.** after all this intensive work of remedying, you reach a point of profound liberation. you've cleared out the decay, established new foundations, and are now free from the old obligations, the inherited burdens. you’re no longer beholden to external authorities or past directives. this line marks a pivot towards autonomy, towards self-direction. you've done the work that was demanded of you, and now you get to choose your own path, to pursue your own, deeper ambitions. it’s about claiming your sovereignty after a period of intense, necessary service.

the relating hexagram, **Restricting (#60)** — 節, is where all this movement eventually leads. the judgment reads: *“limitation. success. galling limitation must not be persevered in.”* this isn't a punitive restriction, but a necessary and wise one. after you’ve cleaned up the decay of Hexagram 18, you naturally establish new boundaries, new structures, new rules for how things will operate. these are the "restrictions" — the healthy limits you put in place to prevent the old problems from recurring. it’s about self-discipline, about creating a framework that supports the new, healthier state you’ve achieved. the warning is important: don’t make these restrictions so rigid or unbearable that they become "galling." find the balance, create limits that serve, not constrain, your new freedom. this is the landing point, the stable ground you reach after the great work of repair.
