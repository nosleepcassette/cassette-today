**Without Falsehood (#25)**
*innocence, heaven, thunder, the creative*

Under heaven, thunder rolls — the image of Without Falsehood describes a moment where the natural order of things is restored, and all elements are in their right place. This is not about forcing or manipulating circumstances, but rather allowing the innate balance of the universe to assert itself. The judgment of this hexagram says: *"Innocence. Supreme success. Perseverance furthers. If someone is not as he should be, He has misfortune, And it does not further him To undertake anything."* This message is about alignment — when we are in harmony with our true nature and the world around us, success follows naturally. However, if we are out of alignment, if our actions or intentions are not genuine, we will encounter obstacles and our endeavors will not yield the desired outcomes.

The hexagram itself names a time of innocence and simplicity, a posture that demands authenticity and a return to one's true nature. It sees the situation as one where the removal of falsehoods and pretenses is necessary for progress. This is a call to purity of intention and action, a reminder that our words and deeds should reflect our deepest truths. Without Falsehood is about cultivating a state of being that is unencumbered by artifice or deception, where one can move through the world with integrity and clarity.

## synthesis
The single card, Seven of Wands, set the tone for the day with a sense of standing firm and defending one's position, suggesting a need to establish boundaries and assert oneself in the face of challenges. The three-card spread, with the past represented by the King of Cups, the present by the Four of Pentacles, and the future by the Page of Swords, shows a psychological arc from emotional depth to material security and then to intellectual curiosity and exploration. The King of Cups indicates a past that was deeply emotional and possibly turbulent, the Four of Pentacles suggests a present that is focused on holding onto what one has, and the Page of Swords points to a future that is inquisitive and analytical.

The hexagram, Without Falsehood, adds a structural layer to this narrative, emphasizing the importance of authenticity and alignment with one's true nature. It suggests that the psychological arc described by the tarot cards is not just about personal growth, but also about returning to a state of innocence and simplicity, unencumbered by the pretenses and falsehoods that we may have accumulated. The tarot and the I Ching see the same thing from different angles — the need for authenticity, integrity, and alignment with one's deepest truths. The tarot provides a more personal, psychological perspective, while the I Ching offers a broader, more philosophical view.

The interaction between the changing lines of the hexagram and the tarot cards adds depth to this interpretation. For instance, the first line, which speaks of innocent behavior bringing good fortune, resonates with the Seven of Wands' message of standing firm in one's truth. The fourth line, which advises perseverance and remaining without blame, complements the Four of Pentacles' focus on security and stability. The fifth line's counsel to use no medicine in an illness incurred through no fault of one's own suggests a trust in natural processes and a caution against forcing solutions, which aligns with the Page of Swords' emphasis on exploration and discovery.

Practically, this means embracing a posture of simplicity, authenticity, and patience. It means recognizing when we are out of alignment and taking steps to return to our true nature, without forcing or manipulating circumstances. It involves trusting in the natural order of things and allowing events to unfold without interference, just as the image of thunder rolling under heaven suggests. The day ahead is about perseverance, innocence, and a deep trust in the universe's ability to restore balance and harmony when we align ourselves with truth and simplicity.
