## hexagram 34: Great Strength

**Great Strength (#34)** — 大壯 (dàzhuàng)

Thunder above Heaven

Thunder over heaven is strength in motion. The judgment asks for perseverance, not a demonstration of how hard you can push. Having the energy to act is not the same as having a good reason to use it.

The image warns against taking a path simply because you can break through it. There is a difference between protecting what matters and trying to force everyone else into your idea of safety. The latter can damage the very belonging you are trying to keep.

### changing line 1

> Power in the toes. Continuing brings misfortune.

Power in the toes is the first impulse to advance. The warning belongs at the beginning, before momentum gets mistaken for permission. Pause before sending the message or making the demand. Ask whether the action will build trust or only discharge your impatience.

### relating hexagram 32: Long Lasting

Thunder above Wind

Long Lasting shifts the question from immediate power to what can be sustained. Choose a practice you can keep: honest conversation, a reasonable boundary, or a regular check-in. Endurance is not a promise to tolerate anything forever.

## the tarot

focus: **Ten of Cups (upright)** · past: **Four of Wands (upright)** · present: **Seven of Swords (reversed)** · future: **Seven of Pentacles (upright)**

Ten of Cups holds the wish for belonging. Four of Wands gives that wish a remembered foundation: celebration and a place to land. Seven of Swords reversed in the present asks what has been hidden or rationalized. Seven of Pentacles in the future asks for a patient assessment of what the work is actually growing.

### what power is for

There are moments when you can feel force gathering before you know what to do with it. You have enough energy to make a declaration, demand an answer, or finally say what you've held back. Great Strength doesn't ask you to deny that energy. It asks whether the direction you give it is worthy of what you care about.

The danger in the judgment is power outrunning judgment. Once something starts moving, momentum can feel like evidence that you're right. But the ability to push through resistance doesn't tell you whether the resistance is a problem to solve, a boundary to respect, or a sign that someone needs more time. Strength has to stay in conversation with what is just.

The image's established order needn't mean obeying every expectation or keeping a dysfunctional peace. Read it as a question about principles: can you tell the truth without humiliating someone? Can you protect your needs without making another person responsible for regulating every feeling you have? Restraint is not the same as making yourself harmless. It is choosing what your strength serves.

Line one puts the decision at the beginning. The toes are already leaning forward, ready to carry the rest of you. Before you act, name the impulse: I want them to admit it. I want uncertainty to end. I want proof that I matter. Then separate that desire from the action you can actually choose. An honest request might serve you better than an ultimatum. If safety is at stake, seek practical support; patience is not a requirement to remain exposed.

Long Lasting offers a different measure of power. What lasts is not necessarily the intensity of a feeling but the direction you can return to as circumstances change. A relationship or practice is built through repeated choices, including revising an agreement that no longer works. Persistence is not an instruction to tolerate mistreatment forever. Choose conduct that remains recognizable as yours tomorrow, not only in the heat of today.

### the cards, one by one

**Ten of Cups, upright — focus.** The wish underneath the reading is a life where affection and shared safety can be felt. The card doesn't promise that everyone agrees or that a family has to look a particular way. Ask what emotional home means to you, and whether the current arrangement makes room for people to be honest without losing their place.

**Four of Wands, upright — past.** There has been a foundation worth remembering: celebration, homecoming, or a moment when you could set something down and enjoy being together. It may be a literal place or simply evidence that connection has been possible. Respect that foundation without demanding a return to a past that cannot be recreated exactly.

**Seven of Swords, reversed — present.** Something concealed, avoided, or rationalized wants attention. That can mean a confession, but it can also mean recognizing the story you have told yourself to avoid a difficult fact. Don't assume the card identifies a culprit. Start with what you know, what you suspect, and what you need to ask. Those are different things.

**Seven of Pentacles, upright — future.** The next phase calls for assessment rather than a verdict delivered in a rush. What has your effort produced? What needs tending, and what isn't responding despite your work? Patience here is active: notice results, adjust your investment, and allow enough time to see whether a change has taken root.

### a practice for the conversation

Write down the fact you need to discuss, the impact it had, and one request the other person can freely answer. Leave out the part where you script their response. Then listen for what is actually offered, not only for what you hoped to hear. A revelation can open a door, but it cannot force the other person to walk through it at your preferred speed.

The Four of Wands and Seven of Pentacles place that conversation between an existing foundation and a future that still needs cultivation. You can honor what was good without using it to erase what hurts. You can ask for repair without promising an outcome before you've seen sustained change. Pay attention to actions over time, including your own.

Belonging is not the absence of friction. It's a condition in which truth can be spoken and boundaries can exist without either being treated as a threat to the whole. This reading doesn't guarantee that every connection can hold that. It offers a way to find out without abandoning your integrity in the process.

## the synthesis

The difficult move is to tell the truth without trying to control the response. Confession is not a lever that guarantees harmony. Let the truth arrive, then give the relationship or project time to show what it can support. Strength becomes useful when it can stay present without taking over.
