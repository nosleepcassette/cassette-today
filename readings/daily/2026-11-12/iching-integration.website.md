**Long Lasting (#32)**
*THUNDER, WIND, THE AROUSING, THE GENTLE*

the strong trigram Thunder is above, the weak trigram Wind below. this hexagram is the inverse of the preceding one. in the latter we have influence, here we have union as an enduring condition. the two images are thunder and wind, which are likewise constantly paired phenomena. the judgment says: "DURATION. Success. No blame. Perseverance furthers. It furthers one to have somewhere to go." duration is a state whose movement is not worn down by hindrances. it is not a state of rest, for mere standstill is regression. duration is rather the self-contained and therefore self-renewing movement of an organized, firmly integrated whole, taking place in accordance with immutable laws and beginning anew at every ending.

the image: "Thunder and wind: the image of DURATION. Thus the superior man stands firm And does not change his direction." thunder rolls, and the wind blows; both are examples of extreme mobility and so are seemingly the very opposite of duration, but the laws governing their appearance and subsidence, their coming and going, endure. in the same way the independence of the superior man is not based on rigidity and immobility of character. he always keeps abreast of the time and changes with it. what endures is the unswerving directive, the inner law of his being, which determines all his actions.

the lower trigram is Wind (the Gentle -- penetrating persistence, wood, the eldest daughter); the upper trigram is Thunder (the Arousing -- shock and sudden movement, the eldest son, spring energy). what arises from below meets what governs from above. one line is changing at position 2.

line 2: "Remorse disappears." the situation is abnormal. a man's force of character is greater than the available material power. thus he might be afraid of allowing himself to attempt something beyond his strength. however, since it is the time of duration, it is possible for him to control his inner strength and so to avoid excess. cause for remorse then disappears.

this movement tends toward Hexagram 62, Little Exceeding (小過): "PREPONDERANCE OF THE SMALL. Success. Perseverance furthers. Small things may be done; great things should not be done. The flying bird brings the message: It is not well to strive upward, It is well to remain below. Great good fortune." exceptional modesty and conscientiousness are sure to be rewarded with success; however, if a man is not to throw himself away, it is important that they should not become empty form and subservience but be combined always with a correct dignity in personal behavior.

## the tarot surface

this morning's card was **Three of Pentacles**: craftsmanship, teamwork, skill, planning, collaboration, quality. it gives the day its first visible instruction: treat today's work as though the quality matters, not because someone is grading you, but because what lasts is built with attention.

the three-card spread: **Ten of Wands** in the past -- burden, responsibility, effort, pressure. a cycle reaching completion under a heavy load. you've been coming from overload, the kind of pressure that comes from saying yes to everything and then realizing the total weight.

**Page of Pentacles** in the present. study, opportunity, practicality, diligence. the page approaches the material world with seriousness, curiosity, willingness to learn. you're in student mode now, not expert mode. the past was carrying the weight of being responsible for everything. the present is learning how to do one thing well.

**Nine of Swords** in the future. anxiety, worry, guilt, mental anguish, fear loops. this is what happens if the pattern continues: the diligent study of the page becomes the trap of overthinking, and the relief you felt from putting down the ten of wands turns into paralysis because now you're second-guessing everything.

## the synthesis

the cards show burden in the past, learning in the present, anxiety ahead if the pattern continues. the hexagram shows the deeper structure: Long Lasting. together they say the question is not about doing more or doing less. it's about finding the rhythm that can be sustained.

duration is not a state of rest. it's self-renewing movement, an organized whole that begins anew at every ending. the ten of wands was not sustainable -- that's why it's in the past. the page of pentacles is the beginning of something that could last, but only if you don't let it collapse into the nine of swords.

line 2 says: remorse disappears. the situation is abnormal -- your force of character is greater than the available material power. you might be afraid of attempting something beyond your strength. but since it is the time of duration, it is possible to control your inner strength and avoid excess. that's the instruction for the page: you don't have to prove your capacity by overcommitting again. you can study, learn, build slowly, and let that be enough.

the relating hexagram, Little Exceeding, reinforces this: small things may be done; great things should not be done. it is not well to strive upward; it is well to remain below. exceptional modesty and conscientiousness are sure to be rewarded with success. the bird should not try to surpass itself and fly into the sun; it should descend to the earth, where its nest is.

so here's the practical move: you're recovering from the ten of wands by learning to work at a sustainable pace. the nine of swords shows up if you let that recovery turn into obsessive second-guessing. the hexagram is saying: find the rhythm that can last. not the sprint, not the collapse -- the durable movement that renews itself. thunder and wind are extreme mobility, but the laws governing their appearance endure. be like that. change with the time, but keep your inner directive steady. small things today. not because you're incapable of great things, but because great things are not what this moment requires.
