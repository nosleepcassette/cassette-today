*perfectionism, boredom, cutting corners, stalled development*

check what the work is actually teaching you. repeating something a hundred times can build skill. it can also build a very efficient route around the thing you need to learn. today's reversed eight asks you to notice the difference before you donate another afternoon to it.

there are two familiar versions of this card: polishing one tiny detail until the rest of the project starves, and rushing through everything because you stopped caring three repetitions ago. they look like opposites. both can happen when your relationship with the work has gone flat. neither is proof that you're lazy or incapable. they are reasons to inspect the conditions, not issue a verdict on your character.

if you've been revising the same message, moving the same pile, or reopening the same unfinished task, stop at the point where your attention slips. what is missing there? a clear standard? an example? enough time? permission to be a beginner? sometimes the next useful step is another attempt. sometimes it's asking someone to show you the part the instructions skipped.

## the thread

craft needs feedback, not just hours. choose one small piece of work and define what finished means before you touch it. a paragraph that says the necessary thing. a repair that holds. a practice session focused on one difficult transition, rather than another complete performance of everything you already know.

then give the result an honest check. don't pretend a shortcut is harmless if someone else will inherit the problem. don't treat an invisible cosmetic flaw as a moral emergency either. quality is care applied where it matters, with a stopping point attached.

there's room here to recover interest by changing the scale. put down the entire imagined future of your skill and make one thing a little better. if the task has genuinely become empty repetition, adjust the method or take a break before resentment becomes the only thing you're practicing.

you don't need to love every minute of doing something well. you do need some way to tell whether the effort is still serving the work — and whether the work is still allowing you to be a person.
