**觀 · guān ䷓**
*wind over earth, contemplation, being seen*

the Chinese name for this hexagram carries two meanings depending on tone: contemplating, and being seen. Wilhelm pictures it as an old watchtower on a hill. it commands a view of the whole countryside, and for the same reason it's a landmark visible for miles. **Watching, hexagram 20**, holds both at once. the lower trigram is earth, receptive and open. the upper trigram is wind, gentle and far-reaching. wind moves over the ground and the grass bends. nobody forces it. the wind just goes where it goes.

the judgment reads: "CONTEMPLATION. The ablution has been made, / But not yet the offering. / Full of trust they look up to him." in the old ritual, the washing came first and the sacrifice came after. Wilhelm calls the moment between them the most sacred of the whole ceremony, the point of deepest concentration. the prep is done. the act hasn't happened yet. that held breath is the hexagram. it isn't a stall. it's the attention that makes the offering mean anything.

the image says: "The wind blows over the earth... Thus the kings of old visited the regions of the world, / Contemplated the people, / And gave them instruction." they looked first, at how people actually lived, and only then spoke. the influence doesn't come from announcements. it comes from what people see you doing.

### the changing lines

**line 1: "Boy like contemplation. / For an inferior man, no blame. / For a superior man, humiliation."** this is looking from a distance without understanding what you're looking at. for someone with no stake in the outcome, harmless. for someone who's supposed to know better, it's an embarrassment. the commentary's fix: see the forces in play as a connected whole instead of grabbing at the surface. this is the line about skimming. glance, nod, move on, call it informed.

**line 5: "Contemplation of my life. / The superior man is without blame."** self-examination, but a specific kind. the commentary is direct that it isn't idle brooding. it's examining the effects you produce. the test isn't how you feel about yourself; it's what your choices are doing to the work and the people around you.

so both moving lines are about the quality of attention. the bottom one is too shallow. the fifth one is aimed correctly: outward, at consequences, instead of inward at mood.

### where it's moving: hexagram 27, Nourishing

those two lines change and the cast becomes **Nourishing (頤 · yí ䷚)**, the picture of an open mouth. its judgment: "Pay heed to the providing of nourishment / And to what a man seeks / To fill his own mouth with." its image: "the superior man is careful of his words / And temperate in eating and drinking." watching turns into feeding. once you've looked properly, the question becomes what you take in and what you put out. if you want to know what someone is like, the commentary says, look at what they nourish.

## the tarot: the surface

the cards today were the surface. hexagram 20 is what's underneath.

**past — Three of Pentacles** was the work. craftsmanship, teamwork, skill, done in a room with other people who could see it. that's the tower half of Watching: being visible because the thing you made actually holds. you have a record. people have watched you build.

**present — Seven of Cups** is the row of visions. imagination, options, fantasy, each cup holding something different and most of them glowing. this is the card line 1 is talking about. it's staring at the possibilities from across the room, taken by the shine, without knowing what's inside any of them. the Seven of Cups isn't wrong to dream. it's wrong to mistake looking for deciding. Watching asks for the other kind of looking: close, connected, focused on consequences. which of these would hold weight the way the Three of Pentacles work did? which one is just pretty?

**future — The Lovers (reversed)** is where shallow looking leads if nobody corrects it. disharmony, misalignment, indecision, a choice that doesn't get made cleanly or gets made against your own values. the hexagram's third line isn't moving today, but it sits right in the middle of the figure: "Contemplation of my life / Decides the choice / Between advance and retreat." the decision the reversed Lovers can't make gets made by examining the effects of your own life, not by staring harder at the cups.

this morning's **Six of Swords** set the undertone: transition, passage, being carried across the water. Watching fits it well. a crossing is a good place to look. there isn't much else to do in the boat, and the shore behind you gets clearer as it gets smaller.

## the sky

no significant transits came through on today's chart. nothing outside to push against, and nothing to blame. the hexagram has the field to itself, which suits a reading about your own attention.

## the synthesis

the cards showed a progression: solid work, then a table full of possibilities, then a choice that might go sideways. the hexagram shows the hinge between them. the I Ching doesn't contradict the tarot. it contextualizes it. the Seven of Cups is a watching problem, and the reversed Lovers is what happens when the watching stays shallow.

practically: you're between the ablution and the offering. the preparation is done. before you choose, look properly. take each option on the table and ask what it would actually do to your days, your work, and the people who watch how you move. that's line 5's test, effects over feelings about effects. throw out the ones that only look good from a distance.

then the turn toward Nourishing: be careful what you feed. information, attention, food, and what you say out loud about the plan before it exists. temperate in all of it.

the tower sees for miles and gets seen for miles. both are true at once, so choose like someone's watching. someone usually is.
