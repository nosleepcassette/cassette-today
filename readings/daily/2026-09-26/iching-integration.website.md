**Nourishing (#27)**
*mountain, over, thunder, keeping still, providing nourishment, corners of the mouth*

at the foot of the mountain, thunder — this is the image of nourishing, an instruction from the earth itself. you stand at the base of something immense, something still, while a deep rumble moves beneath you, a force that stirs and awakens. it’s a moment for quiet attention, for hearing what echoes in the quiet, for watching what truly sustains life. the superior person, the image says, is careful with their words, temperate in their eating and drinking. it’s not about grand gestures, but about the small, deliberate choices that build a life. a reminder that what you take in, and what you put out, forms the very structure of your being.

the judgment arrives direct, almost blunt: *the corners of the mouth. perseverance brings good fortune. pay heed to the providing of nourishment and to what a man seeks to fill his own mouth with.* it’s a phrase that brings you right back to the body, to the most basic act of sustenance. what are you consuming? not just food, but ideas, relationships, information, energy. and what are you offering? what nourishment do you provide to others, and to yourself? this hexagram asks you to look critically at your diet — in every sense of the word. it’s a time to prune the non-essentials, to ensure what remains is truly life-giving. the perseverance here isn't about pushing through, but about consistently making the right choices for your inner and outer well-being. it’s about a steady hand, a thoughtful plate.

nourishing is the hexagram of discernment, of understanding that true care isn't just about abundance, but about quality and intention. it names a time when the roots need tending, when the foundations of your strength are being re-examined. it demands a posture of intentional consumption and mindful giving. you're being asked to observe not just what you *do*, but what you *take in* to fuel that doing. it sees the situation as one where your energy sources, your emotional diet, your intellectual fodder, are all up for review. this isn't a time for casual intake. this is a time for conscious digestion.

the second line is changing today, offering a specific caution: *turning to the summit for nourishment, deviating from the path.* this line points to a misstep, a seeking of sustenance in the wrong direction, or from the wrong source. it’s like looking for water on a mountaintop when the spring is at the base. you’re perhaps reaching for something grand or aspirational, something that *looks* like nourishment from a distance, but isn't actually what you need right now. it's a detour from what's truly accessible and beneficial. this deviation, this seeking of the "summit," can lead you astray, wasting energy on an unsustainable pursuit. it’s a gentle warning to re-evaluate your current strategy for self-care and growth — are you feeding yourself with what you truly need, or what you think you *should* need?

when line two changes, hexagram 27, Nourishing, transforms into hexagram 41, Decreasing (損). this is where the day is headed, the resolution of that initial question about sustenance. the judgment for decreasing is: *decrease combined with sincerity brings about supreme good fortune without blame. one may be persevering in this. it furthers one to undertake something. how is this to be carried out? one may use two small bowls for the sacrifice.* decreasing isn't about deprivation, but about intentional reduction. it’s about shedding what no longer serves, cutting away the excess to reveal the core. the movement from nourishing to decreasing suggests that true nourishment might first require letting go. that "deviating from the path" in line two, that wrong search for sustenance, ultimately leads to a moment where you must actively reduce, rather than simply consume. it's a shift from adding to subtracting, from filling to emptying, all in the service of a deeper, more sincere replenishment.

## synthesis

the morning began with the four of wands, a card of stability, of coming home, of finding a moment of peace and celebration after effort. it suggests a deep longing for security, a wish for things to be settled and clear. but beneath that aspiration, the three-card spread tells a more complex story, one that the hexagram then deepens. the past, the five of wands reversed, indicates conflict either avoided or finally resolving — a turbulent period giving way, perhaps, to that desire for stability. but then, the present, the three of swords: a stark image of heartbreak, of difficult truths piercing through. it’s the pain of insight, the sorrow of reality. and this leads to the future, the ten of wands, a burden carried, a heavy responsibility, a sense of being overwhelmed by the weight of obligation.

the hexagram of nourishing asks you to look at what you’re consuming, what you’re feeding yourself, and the changing line warns against seeking nourishment in the wrong place. this is where the tarot and the i ching align. the three of swords, that piercing truth, is a form of difficult nourishment — it’s essential, but it hurts. the ten of wands, the future burden, could be the direct result of that "deviating from the path" in line two of nourishing. are you taking on too much, consuming too many responsibilities, because you believe they will sustain you, or because you’re looking for validation in the wrong places? the "summit" that line two warns about could be that overwhelming sense of duty, that constant push to achieve, that ultimately leaves you burdened rather than nourished.

the movement to hexagram 41, decreasing, is the crucial turning point. it’s the antidote to the ten of wands, the balm for the three of swords. if you are carrying too much, if your present is marked by painful truths, and your future by overwhelm, then decreasing is the path forward. it’s not about giving up, but about a sincere reduction — cutting back on what drains you, on what doesn't truly nourish you, even if it feels like a duty. the four of wands' longing for home and stability can only be met when you are discerning about what you allow into your life, and what you choose to let go of. this moment asks you to shed, to simplify, to make room for what truly sustains you, rather than continuing to seek fulfillment in what only adds to your load. pay attention to what you consume, to what you carry, and have the courage to decrease with sincerity. it's how you come back to yourself. it's how you find the true spring at the foot of the mountain.
