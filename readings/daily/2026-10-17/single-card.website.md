*intuition, dreams, uncertainty, illusion, subconscious, mystery*

the moon asks you to slow down before you turn a feeling into a fact. something can feel charged without its meaning being settled. a delayed reply, an unfamiliar mood, a dream that follows you into breakfast — all of it can be worth noticing. none of it has to become a verdict before you've had water.

this is a card of partial visibility. you can see enough of the path to take a careful step, but not enough to explain the whole landscape. that isn't a personal failure. the trouble starts when you fill the unlit parts with whichever story hurts most, then call the story intuition because it arrived with a strong pulse.

intuition deserves better treatment than that. listen to the sensation, then ask what it actually tells you. “i feel uneasy” is useful information. “therefore i know what everyone is hiding” is a much bigger claim. the moon holds dreams, images, and emotional undercurrents; it doesn't issue a warrant to search somebody else's motives.

there's room for imagination here, too. write down the strange image. make something from the feeling without requiring it to explain itself. a poem can hold contradictory truths more comfortably than an argument can. you don't have to interrogate every tender thing until it produces a practical answer.

## keep the question open

if a decision can wait, give it a little daylight. if it can't, choose the smallest reversible action available. ask one plain question instead of sending the paragraph that assumes the answer. check the appointment time. read the actual message again. ordinary verification is not the enemy of mystery; it keeps mystery from eating your afternoon.

and if you need quiet, take it without making quiet into a punishment. lower the volume, leave yourself a note about what's unresolved, and come back when your body isn't doing all the talking. you can respect a feeling without handing it the steering wheel. tonight, let “i don't know yet” be a complete sentence.
