**Increasing (#42)**  
*wind over thunder, the gentle, increase, growth through right action*

Wind moves over thunder. You cannot see the wind directly, but you can see what it changes — leaves turning, branches bending, weather arriving before anyone has named it. Thunder breaks the stillness underneath. Together, they describe increase as a living force: movement above, pressure below, a situation gaining energy because something has finally begun to shift.

The image is not an instruction to accumulate indiscriminately. It says: notice what is already alive, then give it better conditions. The wind spreads. The thunder awakens. Increase comes through circulation, attention, and timely action. The superior person, seeing what is good, imitates it; seeing faults, removes them. No grand reinvention. No ceremonial self-improvement project with a new notebook and a doomed color-coding system. You identify what works, then do more of that. You identify what harms you, then stop feeding it.

The Judgment says: *“Increase. It furthers one to undertake something. It furthers one to cross the great water.”* This is permission to move. Not recklessly, and not because motion is automatically virtuous, but because the conditions favor meaningful effort. Crossing the great water means committing to something whose outcome cannot be controlled from the shore. You do not get to keep every option open while also claiming you are changing your life. At some point, the boat leaves.

Hexagram 42 names a time when giving and receiving are being recalibrated. Something is available to grow, but it needs participation. The situation rewards generosity, correction, and practical courage. It asks you to become a better steward of your own energy — what you give attention to, what you repeat, what you allow to occupy the center of the room.

This is not expansion for its own sake. It is increase in alignment with life. What deserves more of you? What has been taking more than it returns? Those questions are part of the reading.

**Line 5** says: *“If in truth you have a kind heart, ask not.”* The line is not telling you to become silent, passive, or mysteriously noble about your needs. It is pointing to a form of generosity that does not keep checking the receipt. When the heart is genuinely aligned, you do not need to bargain with the universe before acting. You do not need a guarantee that kindness will be rewarded, reciprocated, or correctly understood.

Today, this line offers a gift and a warning. The gift is trust in your own good intention. You can do the right thing without turning it into a referendum on your worth. The warning is against using generosity as a disguised demand. If you give, give cleanly. If you need something, ask plainly. Do not perform selflessness while waiting for someone to decode the invoice hidden inside it.

Line 5 sits at the center of Increase, where inner motive becomes outer consequence. Its kindness is not sentimental. It is disciplined. You know what you are doing, and you do not need to make the gesture louder than the truth behind it.

The primary hexagram becomes **Hexagram 27 — Nourishing (頤)**. This is the destination of Increase. The energy that begins as expansion moves toward discernment about nourishment — what enters through the mouth, what leaves through it, what you consume, what you say, and what you seek to fill yourself with.

Hexagram 27 is called *The Corners of the Mouth*. Its Judgment says: *“Perseverance brings good fortune. Pay heed to the providing of nourishment and to what a man seeks to fill his own mouth with.”* The movement is from increasing life to feeding life well. Primary → relating: first, make the living thing larger; then, ask whether you are nourishing the right thing.

That is where the day is going. Increase is not the final answer. It is the opening move. The question underneath it is what your growth will consume, and what it will be made to consume you.

The tarot sees the inner weather. The I Ching sees the structure underneath it. They are describing the same moment from different depths.

The Nine of Swords reversed says the mind is beginning to loosen its grip on catastrophe. The Queen of Swords says that relief will require honesty, not reassurance. The Two of Swords reversed says the cost of maintaining ambiguity has become higher than the cost of knowing. Hexagram 42 agrees, but adds motion: once you see what is good, imitate it; once you see the fault, remove it. Increase does not ask you to wait until you feel fearless. It asks you to participate in the correction.

The Sagittarius Sun in the twelfth house complicates this. Part of the growth is invisible. You may not be able to prove that you are changing because the most important shift is happening below the social surface — in what you release, what you forgive yourself for, and what no longer gets to define your identity. The Leo Moon in the eighth house makes the emotional material louder anyway. You want to be seen in the places where you are most exposed. You may also want to dramatize the wound a little, because being witnessed can feel like proof that it mattered. Fine. Just do not confuse intensity with nourishment.

Mercury in Scorpio gives the day its knife. Ask the question you have been circling. Notice what your future plans reveal about your actual desires. Notice which friendships, communities, or imagined outcomes leave you clearer, and which ones require you to keep abandoning your own perception.

The practical instruction is simple: increase what makes you more honest, more alive, and more capable of choosing. Reduce what keeps you frightened, fogged, or performing generosity in exchange for love. Make one clean move across the water. Then pay attention to what you are feeding with it.
