**Remedying (#18)**
*mountain, wind, keeping still*

the wind blows low on the mountain — this is the image of decay, of things slowly eroding, wearing down. it’s not a sudden catastrophe, you know. it’s the quiet creep of neglect, the slow unraveling of something once solid. the mountain is still, but the wind is working on it, chipping away, revealing what’s underneath. this image tells you that the superior person doesn't just watch this happen. they stir things up. they strengthen the spirit, not just their own, but the collective. it’s an active engagement with decline, a refusal to let things just fall apart.

the judgment is direct: *WORK ON WHAT HAS BEEN SPOILED. Has supreme success. It furthers one to cross the great water. Before the starting point, three days. After the starting point, three days.* this isn't a suggestion, it's an imperative. you have to deal with the rot, the neglect, the things that have gone bad. the good news is, there's supreme success on the other side of this work. it’s not easy, but it’s worth it. crossing the great water means taking on something formidable, something that requires courage and commitment. and the timing? before, three days. after, three days. it’s a period of preparation, then a period of integration. it tells you this isn't a quick fix, but a process with its own rhythm. you’re in a moment of deep, necessary repair.

hexagram 18 names a time when the consequences of past errors, oversights, or generational patterns are finally surfacing. it’s not about blaming, it’s about observing the effects. what’s broken isn’t necessarily your fault, but it’s absolutely your responsibility to address it now. this posture demands you look closely at the foundations, the assumptions, the things you’ve been relying on that might be quietly disintegrating. it sees a situation where the external structure—the mountain—is being eroded by internal forces—the wind working its way into the cracks. this isn't a time for building new things on shaky ground. it’s a time for shoring up, for cleaning out, for deep restoration. you’re being called to mend what’s been left unattended.

line 2 speaks to *setting right what has been spoiled by the mother. One must not be too persevering.* this refers to the foundational, often unconscious, patterns or emotional legacies that have created a mess. "spoiled by the mother" points to issues rooted in nurture, early environment, emotional security, or the very fabric of your being. it’s about the inherited emotional baggage, the default settings. the caution here is crucial: don't be *too* persevering. this isn't about brute-forcing a solution. it’s about understanding the delicate nature of these deep-seated issues. sometimes, the most effective way to remedy is to step back, to allow, to release, rather than to push harder. you’re asked to gently untangle, not rip out.

the third line shifts focus to *setting right what has been spoiled by the father. There will be a little remorse. No great blame.* if the second line was about the internal, the third is often about the external, the structural, the authoritative. "spoiled by the father" refers to issues rooted in outer structures, rules, ambition, or the way you navigate the world. it could be about societal expectations, career paths, or the way you’ve been taught to exert authority or achieve success. there will be a little remorse, yes—a recognition of past choices or paths that didn't serve. you might feel a pang of regret for not seeing it sooner, or for adhering to a faulty system. but the I Ching is clear: *no great blame*. this isn't about self-flagellation. it’s about acknowledging the error, learning from it, and moving on to fix it. these two lines together tell you that the decay you’re experiencing has roots in both your inherited emotional landscape and your external structures, and both require different, careful approaches to repair.

**Falling Away (#23)**
*splitting apart, no furthering*

when the changing lines complete their movement, Hexagram 18 transforms into Hexagram 23, Falling Away. this is where the day is going, the destination of all this remedial work. if you’ve been working to fix things, the logical outcome is that some things simply cannot be fixed. some things *must* fall away. the judgment of Falling Away is stark: *SPLITTING APART. IT does not further one To go anywhere.* this isn't a gentle pruning; it’s a stripping back. imagine the mountain again, but now the wind has scoured it bare. all the loose soil, the dead branches, the weak rock—it’s gone. there's nothing left but the essential core.

this hexagram asks you to recognize what is beyond repair, what is no longer serving, what needs to be removed for true renewal to occur. it’s a moment of necessary retreat, a time to shed layers. "it does not further one to go anywhere" means this is not a time for outward action, for pushing forward, for seeking new ground. it’s a time for internal consolidation, for letting go, for recognizing that sometimes, the only way forward is to stop, stand still, and allow the stripping-away process to complete itself. the work of remedying in #18 prepares you for this essential clearing in #23. it’s a profound act of simplification, a return to the bare bones before anything new can truly begin.

you are feeling the cold, aren't you? that Five of Pentacles casts a long shadow, a sense of being on the outside looking in, feeling the pinch of hardship or isolation. it's not just physical; it’s a deep, raw ache of being overlooked, of struggling. this is the feeling that Hexagram 18 is asking you to address. that cold spot, that sense of lack, is a symptom of something spoiled, something decayed at its root. the I Ching tells you this isn't a permanent state, but a call to action—a call to work on what has been left to rot.

in the past, you were the Queen of Wands—radiant, confident, utterly self-possessed, full of creative fire. that was a time of knowing your worth, commanding your space. but something has shifted. now, you find yourself in the Seven of Wands, defending that very position. you're standing on higher ground, yes, but you’re fending off challenges, holding your boundaries, fighting for what you believe is yours. this present moment of defense is the immediate, visceral response to the decay the I Ching names. you're feeling the pressure, the erosion, and you’re pushing back. the "spoiled by the mother" and "spoiled by the father" lines of Hexagram 18 suggest that this defense might be against both old emotional patterns that undermine your confidence, and external structures or expectations that are now proving unstable. you're battling the consequences of past neglect, whether yours or someone else's.

the thread here is about identifying what exactly you’re defending. is it worth saving? or is part of this defense actually clinging to something that needs to fall away, as Hexagram 23 suggests? the work of remedying isn't just about patching things up; it’s about discerning what needs mending and what needs to be stripped bare. perhaps the Five of Pentacles feeling is a result of trying to hold onto too much, of carrying burdens that are no longer serving you. the "supreme success" of Hexagram 18 comes from confronting this decay head-on, not just defending against its symptoms.

the future, the Knight of Cups, offers a profound shift. after the defense, after the difficult work of remedying and the necessary stripping away, there's an opening for a new, gentler emotional landscape. this knight comes bearing feelings, intuition, a tender offering. it suggests that once you’ve done the hard work of addressing the decay, of letting things fall away, you’ll be open to a more authentic, heart-centered experience. this isn't about fighting anymore. it's about receiving, about connecting. the isolation of the Five of Pentacles gives way to a sensitive, empathetic engagement with the world. the work you do now, the uncomfortable truths you face, are clearing the path for this emotional renewal. you are being asked to clean house, to mend what's salvageable, and to release what's not, so that you can step into a space of genuine feeling, unburdened by the past's slow rot.
