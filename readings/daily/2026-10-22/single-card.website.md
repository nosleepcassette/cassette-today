*transition, passage, healing, support, distance*

you can make the right move and still feel sad about making it. the six of swords doesn't demand a celebratory exit. it describes passage away from turbulence, with enough room in the boat for whatever you haven't finished feeling.

that matters if you've been treating grief as proof that you should go back. missing the familiar isn't the same as wanting its conditions restored. a routine can be uncomfortable and still feel safer than an unfamiliar option. you can know you need distance and hesitate when it's time to create it. there isn't a contradiction to solve there. there's a crossing to make at a human pace.

look for the practical version of calmer water. fewer interruptions. a conversation moved out of a public argument. an afternoon somewhere you aren't expected to keep explaining yourself. sometimes the passage is a major change; sometimes it's turning off a thread long enough to hear your own thoughts again. the scale doesn't determine whether the relief counts.

## the thread

the six includes support. you don't have to confuse independence with doing every part of a transition unaided. someone can help you find the route, carry a bag, or sit with you while you make the call. accepting that help doesn't give them ownership of the destination.

be specific about what would make the next stretch manageable. “i need everything to be different” is an understandable feeling, but it doesn't tell your hands what to do. “i need the document, a ride, and an hour without messages” gives the day something to work with. make one arrangement that reduces friction instead of requiring a whole new supply of courage.

this card doesn't promise that distance instantly becomes healing. you may arrive somewhere quieter and only then notice how tired you are. give that adjustment time. don't fill every new inch of space just because silence feels unfamiliar at first.

today, choose a direction that asks less of your defenses. take what you need, leave room for help, and let sadness come along without making it the navigator. you can be on your way while part of you is still saying goodbye.
