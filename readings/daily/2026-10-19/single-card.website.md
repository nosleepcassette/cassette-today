## the card

**Three of Swords**

*heartbreak, sorrow, separation, truth, grief, pain*

the three of swords names the hurt without handing you a deadline for turning it into wisdom. if something stings today, you don't have to decorate it with gratitude before you're allowed to acknowledge it. disappointment can be disappointing. a clear answer can still be an answer you didn't want.

this card isn't a prediction that somebody will betray you. it's a place to put the pain that already needs your attention, whether that's a difficult conversation, an ending, or the gap between what you hoped and what actually happened. clarity hurts sometimes because it removes the version of events you were using to keep going. that doesn't make clarity cruel. it makes the adjustment real.

be careful about what you add to the first wound. “this didn't work” is one truth. “nothing will ever work for me” is an entire prosecution built on top of it. the sword can cut through denial without being turned against your worth. you can name a fact precisely and decline the extra punishment.

## make room without making a monument

pick a container for the feeling. a page you don't send, a conversation with someone who listens, a walk without rehearsing the argument the whole way. grief doesn't become more legitimate when it occupies every available surface. giving it a place can help you return to the rest of the day without pretending it isn't there.

if another person is involved, stick to what happened and what you need now. you're allowed to say a comment hurt. you're allowed to ask for space. you don't need a complete theory of their character to decide that a particular behavior isn't something you can keep accepting.

and keep the ordinary care embarrassingly ordinary. eat lunch. answer the necessary message, not every message. put clean water next to the place you're sitting. sorrow can make the practical world feel distant, but you still live in it. there is no prize for being so articulate about heartbreak that you forget you have a body to bring through the afternoon.
