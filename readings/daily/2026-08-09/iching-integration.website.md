**Nourishing (#27)** 
*MOUNTAIN, THUNDER, KEEPING STILL*

The image of the hexagram describes a scene at the foot of a mountain, where thunder is present — this is the image of providing nourishment. Literally, it's a place of stillness, where the energy of thunder is not moving, but its presence is felt. As an instruction, this image suggests that the act of nourishment is not just about giving or taking, but about being in the right place, at the right time, with the right posture. 

_"The corners of the mouth. Perseverance brings good fortune. Pay heed to the providing of nourishment and to what a man seeks to fill his own mouth with."_ This judgment is not just about eating or feeding others, but about the way we approach our own needs, and the needs of those around us. It's about being mindful of what we're putting into our bodies, and what we're putting out into the world. The phrase "the corners of the mouth" is a beautiful and strange image — it's a reminder that our words, our speech, are a form of nourishment, and that we should be careful about what we say, and how we say it.

The hexagram itself names a time of quiet, of stillness, of being in a place where the energy is not about action, but about reception. It demands a posture of humility, of openness, of being willing to receive what is being offered. It sees that the situation is not just about what we're doing, but about how we're doing it — are we coming from a place of scarcity, or a place of abundance? Are we trying to fill our own mouths, or are we open to being fed by others?

## synthesis
The single card, the Ace of Cups, set the morning's tone — a sense of new beginnings, of fresh emotional waters. The three-card spread showed a psychological arc — from the past, where we were carrying our own burdens, to the present, where we're in a place of quiet, of stillness, and to the future, where we're releasing our burdens, and finding a sense of freedom. The hexagram shows the structural layer underneath both — a reminder that the act of nourishment is not just about what we're doing, but about how we're doing it.

The tarot and the I Ching see the same thing from different angles — both are pointing to the importance of being mindful of our own needs, and the needs of those around us. The tarot is showing us the emotional, psychological landscape, while the I Ching is showing us the deeper, structural layer. Where they confirm each other is in the importance of stillness, of quiet, of being in the right place, at the right time. Where the I Ching complicates the tarot is in its reminder that the act of nourishment is not just about what we're doing, but about how we're doing it — are we coming from a place of scarcity, or a place of abundance?

Practically, this means that today, we should carry a sense of humility, of openness, of being willing to receive what is being offered. We should be mindful of our own needs, and the needs of those around us, and approach them with a sense of care, of attention. We should be careful about what we're putting into our bodies, and what we're putting out into the world. And we should trust that the act of nourishment is not just about what we're doing, but about how we're doing it — that the universe is always providing, always nourishing, and that we just need to be in the right place, at the right time, with the right posture.
