**Following (#17)**
LAKE, THUNDER, THE JOYOUS

thunder in the middle of the lake — it’s a sound that resonates, deep and echoing, contained within the vastness of the water. it’s not chaos, it’s a force moving within a receptive container. the image of following, the text says, means that you go indoors at nightfall for rest and recuperation. you find your rhythm. you align yourself with the natural cycle of activity and stillness, of engagement and retreat. it's not about blindly chasing after every whim, but about understanding when to move with the current and when to step back, to replenish your energy so you can follow again with clear purpose.

*following has supreme success. perseverance furthers. no blame.* this isn't about being a sheep, it’s about intelligent alignment. real success comes from knowing when to lead and when to lend your energy to a larger movement, a greater truth. it demands a quiet persistence, a steadfastness in your chosen path. there's no fault in this — in fact, it's the most effective way to navigate certain moments. you’re not surrendering your will; you’re directing it with wisdom.

this hexagram names a time when your power comes not from solitary initiation, but from intelligent adaptation. it’s a posture of receptivity, but not passivity. there’s a current you’re meant to join, a principle to uphold, or a person whose vision genuinely resonates with yours. the situation calls for you to observe, to understand the momentum around you, and to choose where your loyalty and energy will be most effectively placed. it’s about influence, yes, but also about being influenced by what is truly worthy of your attention.

the first line is changing, telling you that the very standard you're meant to follow is shifting underfoot. it’s not a time to cling to outdated maps. *the standard is changing. perseverance brings good fortune. to go out of the door in company produces deeds.* this means that while the old rules might not apply, a steadfast heart will still guide you. more importantly, you’re not meant to do this alone. finding your people, connecting with those who share your evolving vision, that’s where the real action happens. your deeds, your impact, will be amplified by the company you keep. it’s about finding the right tribe for the new landscape.

the second line is also in motion, offering a crucial distinction in *what* you choose to follow. *if one clings to the little boy, one loses the strong man.* this is a sharp, clear warning. the "little boy" represents fleeting desires, superficial comforts, or immature pursuits. it’s the easy, immediate gratification that distracts. the "strong man," on the other hand, embodies a deeper, more substantial, and enduring path — a principle, a mature goal, a person of true integrity. if you chase what’s trivial or temporary, you forfeit the chance to build genuine strength and lasting value. this line, alongside the first, tells you not only that the rules are changing and you need new company, but that you must exercise extreme discernment in *who* and *what* you follow into this new phase. don’t just jump on the next bandwagon because the old one broke.

when these lines shift, the hexagram of Following transforms into **Exhausting (#47) — K'un 困**. this isn't a dead end; it's a destination, a potential consequence, and a further layer of understanding. *oppression. success. perseverance. the great man brings about good fortune. no blame. when one has something to say, it is not believed.* this is where the journey of following can lead if discernment is lacking, or if the path itself becomes incredibly challenging. it speaks to a state of being hemmed in, feeling unheard, possibly depleted. the arc from Following to Exhaustion suggests that the choices you make in aligning yourself — who you follow, what principles you uphold — directly determine whether this state of oppression becomes an insurmountable obstacle or a trial you overcome with integrity. even in exhaustion, the text affirms success is possible through perseverance, especially for the "great man" who maintains their inner truth. it asks: what are you following now that might lead to such a testing point, and how will you carry your integrity through it?

## synthesis

the morning’s single card, the three of cups, sets a tone of joy, reunion, and shared celebration. it’s about coming together, lifting each other up, finding happiness in community. this resonates deeply with hexagram 17’s call to "go out of the door in company" and the very nature of following a shared path. you’re meant to experience this moment with others, to revel in collective achievement or simple camaraderie.

the I Ching weaves a structural layer through these tarot insights. you’ve reached the ten of pentacles, a place of stable, communal success. this is a testament to the "perseverance that furthers" mentioned in hexagram 17’s judgment. the three of cups confirms the celebratory, communal aspect of this current stability. but what happens next? the changing lines in Following are crucial here. line 1 says the standard is changing, and you need to find new company. the page of pentacles is that new beginning, that new path to explore. you’re being invited to follow something fresh, something that requires a new kind of diligence.

line 2 delivers a potent warning: "if one clings to the little boy, one loses the strong man." you’ve achieved the "strong man" of the ten of pentacles — this established security. the danger now is becoming complacent, clinging to superficial comforts or distractions (the "little boy") instead of applying your grounded wisdom to the new opportunity presented by the page of pentacles. don’t let the ease of your current stability blind you to the need for continued, discerning action.

the relating hexagram, Exhaustion, serves as a powerful cautionary tale. you’ve built something significant. but if your choices in "following" the new path are undiscerning, if you chase fleeting pleasures or allow yourself to be led astray, you risk arriving at a place of feeling hemmed in, unheard, or depleted. the key is to apply the same perseverance that brought you to the ten of pentacles to this new, perhaps more humble, beginning. the "great man" in Exhaustion still finds fortune through perseverance, even when unheard. this means maintaining your integrity and a clear sense of purpose, regardless of external circumstances.

your task now is to celebrate your current abundance, yes, but to remain keenly aware of what you choose to follow next. you have a solid foundation. the new opportunity is practical and demands your focused attention. don't squander your hard-won stability by chasing superficialities. lean into the wisdom of your roots, trust your discernment, and apply your energy to what truly matters. your power lies in choosing wisely, in aligning yourself with principles and people that genuinely build upon your legacy, rather than diminish it.
