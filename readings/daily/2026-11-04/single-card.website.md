## the card

**Five of Cups (upright) — focus**

*loss, grief, regret, disappointment*

Something has not gone the way you hoped, and the Five of Cups does not ask you to dress that up as a lesson before you have felt it. The card looks at what has spilled: a relationship that changed, an opportunity that didn't come through, or the smaller disappointment that landed harder because you were already carrying plenty. You don't have to prove that the loss was large enough to deserve your attention.

There is a difference between telling the truth about what happened and making it the whole story of what remains. This card holds both. Some cups are overturned; some are still standing. The ones that remain do not replace what was lost. They are evidence that the loss is not the only thing in the room.

If regret is taking up space, notice whether it is giving you useful information or putting you on trial. You might owe someone an apology, or you might be replaying a decision made with information you no longer have to live without. Those are different problems. One can be addressed. The other needs some tenderness and a stopping point.

## what remains

Take an honest inventory today. Name the thing you miss without immediately adding a silver lining. Then name one source of support that still exists: a person you can call, a task you can set down, or a place where you can be quiet without explaining yourself. If support is hard to see, that doesn't mean you're failing at gratitude. Grief narrows the frame.

The upright Five is not a command to move on by tonight. It's permission to turn your head when you are ready, without pretending the spilled cups were never important. You can carry disappointment and still accept what is offered. You can be sad and still make dinner, answer one message, or ask for help. None of those actions cancels the grief; they give you somewhere to stand while it passes through.
