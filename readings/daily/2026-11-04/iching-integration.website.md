## hexagram 6: Contention

**Contention (#6)** — 訟 (sòng)  
*Heaven above Water; conflict, obstruction, restraint*

```text
6  ───────
5  ───────
4  ───────  changing
3  ─── ───  changing
2  ───────
1  ─── ───  changing
```

Heaven rises while water moves downward. In the image of Contention, the two movements pull apart rather than finding a shared course. That is a useful way to understand a disagreement without making either person into a villain: two aims can be real, sincerely held, and difficult to reconcile. The problem is what happens when the need to prevail becomes larger than the original need.

The judgment recognizes sincerity and obstruction. It favors a cautious halt halfway over pursuing a dispute to its bitter end, advises seeking a capable and impartial figure, and warns against crossing the great water. This isn't an instruction to abandon every claim or endure harm quietly. It is a warning that the scale of an undertaking matters, and that pressing forward while the conditions are adversarial may make the crossing more dangerous.

The image looks earlier in the process: consider the beginning of a transaction carefully. What was actually agreed? What was assumed? What needs to be put in writing before resentment does the work of filling the gaps? Good beginnings do not eliminate every conflict, but they can make a disagreement easier to understand and less expensive to repair.

### changing line 1: don't perpetuate the affair

The first line says that if the affair is not prolonged, there may still be a little gossip, but the outcome can be favorable. Walking away from an emerging dispute does not guarantee that everyone will describe your choice generously. Someone may misunderstand, have the last word, or tell an incomplete story. The line asks whether the cost of correcting every version is worth keeping the fight alive.

This is not advice to ignore a serious violation. It is a scale question. A minor irritation may be allowed to end; a material dispute may need records, boundaries, or professional support. The useful distinction is between the action needed to protect yourself and the extra rounds of argument that only keep the injury active.

### changing line 3: live from what has been earned

The third line speaks of nourishment from established virtue, perseverance through danger, and doing work in service without seeking credit. Its concern is not that recognition never matters. It is that a scramble for prestige can pull attention away from what is genuinely yours: the skill you developed, the work you completed, and the principles you can keep practicing even when someone else receives the applause.

If credit or status has become part of a conflict, separate the practical issue from the need to win admiration. You may need fair attribution or payment. You do not need to convince every observer of your superiority. Let the work be clear, document what matters, and avoid making your peace depend entirely on someone else's verdict.

### changing line 4: change your position

The fourth line describes turning back, changing one's attitude, and finding peace through perseverance. Its commentary makes a sharper point: a person might have the power to prevail over a weaker opponent and still lack a sound reason to keep fighting. The ability to win is not proof that the fight is justified.

Ask where your own position deserves revision. That does not require assuming all blame. It might mean acknowledging a fact you missed, withdrawing a demand you cannot defend, or changing how you pursue a legitimate concern. Peace here is not a performance of being agreeable. It comes from choosing a position you can live with after the adrenaline fades.

### relating hexagram 9: Little Accumulation

**Little Accumulation (#9)** — Wind above Heaven

```text
6  ───────
5  ───────
4  ─── ───
3  ───────
2  ───────
1  ───────
```

Lines one, three, and four change into Little Accumulation. Its image is dense cloud without rain: conditions are gathering, but the full release has not come. Small restraint, steady preparation, and attention to how you conduct yourself can be more useful than trying to force a complete settlement today. A modest agreement or a pause with a clear return date is still a real step.

## the tarot

**Five of Cups (upright) — focus** holds the grief beneath the day's questions. Something has spilled, and what remains does not erase what was lost. This card gives the disagreement an emotional context without claiming that every conflict comes from the same wound.

**Five of Wands (upright) — past** shows friction among competing drives. Some of that pressure may have clarified what you stand for, but it may also have trained you to expect every difference to become a contest. **Strength (upright) — present** offers another response: meet intensity with courage and compassion rather than domination. A steady boundary can contain more power than the loudest argument.

**The Chariot (upright) — future** points toward directed movement, not automatic victory. Its opposing forces need a destination. The question is whether the next action takes you toward what you value or merely keeps you moving inside the dispute.

## the synthesis

Contention puts a brake beside the Chariot. Direction matters, but so does the decision not to cross a dangerous stretch simply because you are determined. Strength makes that pause possible; the Five of Cups lets you admit why the situation hurts; the Five of Wands shows the pattern of friction you don't have to repeat unchanged.

Take one disagreement and write down three things: what you need, what outcome would actually meet that need, and what an unnecessary escalation would look like. If the issue is serious, seek appropriate support rather than treating restraint as silence. If the issue is small, allow the possibility that not answering again is a complete action.

Little Accumulation does not promise a dramatic resolution. It points toward the small, deliberate measure that keeps you aligned while the larger conditions are still forming. You can leave the argument unfinished and still take your own needs seriously.
