**Encountering (#44)**
*heaven, wind, the creative*

under heaven, wind. it’s the image of encountering, of coming to meet. think about wind under heaven — it spreads, it permeates, it touches everything without asking permission. it's how the prince acts when he sends out his commands, reaching into all four corners. something new is arriving, something pervasive, something that touches you whether you invite it or not.

the judgment warns you directly: *“COMING TO MEET. The maiden is powerful. One should not marry such a maiden.”* this isn't about a literal person, not really. it’s about a powerful, alluring, perhaps even overwhelming force that presents itself. it could be an idea, a new connection, a sudden opportunity, or even an old pattern re-emerging with renewed vigor. it looks promising, maybe even inevitable, but the warning is clear: do not commit fully. do not let it take root in the deepest parts of you. it carries an imbalance, a danger in its very power that makes complete integration unwise. this hexagram names a time of vigilance, when a subtle but potent influence starts to make itself known, demanding a strategic, cautious response rather than an open embrace.

the first line changes, telling you: *“It must be checked with a brake of bronze. Perseverance brings good fortune. If one lets it take its course, one experiences misfortune. Even a lean pig has it in him to rage around.”* this is about early intervention, about setting boundaries immediately. don’t underestimate the small, seemingly insignificant impulse or the subtle shift in energy. it might seem harmless, a "lean pig," but if you let it run wild, it *will* cause trouble. apply the brake, firmly and early. maintain your ground. this isn't about fighting every battle, but about choosing your battles before they become wars.

the second line shifts, saying: *“There is a fish in the tank. No blame. Does not further guests.”* here, the powerful force is contained. you’ve acknowledged its presence, you see it, but you're keeping it at arm's length. it’s in the tank, not in your bed. you're not engaging with it fully, not letting it influence your inner circle or your plans. it’s a neutral, watchful stance. there’s no blame in this distance, no need to feel guilty for not welcoming everything that comes knocking. some things are best observed, not integrated.

and the third line changes, noting: *“And walking comes hard. If one is mindful of the danger,”* this suggests a period where progress feels slow, where moving forward is a struggle. it’s not a smooth path, because you’re actively resisting, actively discerning. the difficulty itself is a reminder to stay mindful, to keep the inherent danger of this encounter at the forefront of your awareness. you’re moving with intention, with caution, rather than being swept along by the current. the resistance is a form of protection.

when these lines change, when you apply the brakes, contain the influence, and move with caution, hexagram 44, Encountering, transforms into hexagram 25, Without Falsehood (無妄). this is your destination, the landing point. *“INNOCENCE. Supreme success. Perseverance furthers. If someone is not as he should be, He has misfortune, And it does not further him To undertake anything.”* this is a call to authenticity, to acting with pure intention and integrity. it’s about shedding pretense and returning to your true, unadulterated self. by carefully navigating the powerful encounter of hexagram 44—by not marrying the powerful maiden—you arrive at a place of supreme success that comes from being utterly genuine. it’s the reward for your vigilance: a foundation built on truth, where you can move forward without falsehood.

## synthesis
