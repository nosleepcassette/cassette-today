**Restricting (#60)**
*WATER, LAKE, THE ABYSMAL, THE JOYOUS*

a lake occupies limited space. when more water comes into it, it overflows. therefore limits must be set for the water. the image is water above lake: the inexhaustible meeting a defined capacity. the chinese word for limitation means the joints that divide a bamboo stalk — natural divisions, not arbitrary restrictions. in ordinary life it means thrift, setting fixed limits on expenditure. in the moral sphere it means the fixed limits the superior man sets upon his actions: loyalty, disinterestedness, the boundaries that make freedom possible.

the judgment is a paradox: "LIMITATION. Success. Galling limitation must not be persevered in." limits work. they're effective. they create success. but they can't be taken too far, or they become oppressive. if you live economically in normal times, you're prepared for times of want. if you're sparing, you save yourself from humiliation. but if you try to impose galling limitations on yourself, it's injurious. and if you go too far in imposing them on others, they'll rebel. so there's a limit to limitation itself. this is the work: finding the measure, the joint, the place where structure supports life without strangling it.

the hexagram is asking you to look at where you're setting boundaries — with your time, your energy, your resources, your expectations. are those boundaries sustainable? are they creating the conditions for growth, or are they choking it? because the line between discipline and deprivation is real, and this hexagram is insisting you understand the difference.

---

line one says: "Not going out of the door and the courtyard Is without blame." you're at the beginning. there are obstacles, insurmountable limitations. the move here is to stop. don't go beyond the limits set for you. if you stay within bounds, you accumulate energy, and when the proper time comes, you'll act with great force. this is about discretion, about knowing where to hold back so you don't waste yourself on a premature push. it's strategic restraint, not cowardice.

line four counters with: "Contented limitation. Success." this is the natural limit, the one that doesn't require grinding effort. water flows downhill — that's a limitation, but it's not a struggle. when the limitation is a natural one, it leads to success because you're not fighting against it. you're working with it. the energy that would be consumed in resistance is applied wholly to the benefit of the matter at hand. this is the sweet spot: limitation that feels right, that actually serves the work, that doesn't cost you more than it gives.

these two changing lines are showing you the full range. line one is about accepting imposed limitations, knowing when to stop, conserving energy. line four is about finding the limitations that are actually natural, that work with your grain instead of against it. together they're asking: which limits are you fighting, and which ones are you meant to accept?

---

when these lines complete their motion, hexagram 60 transforms into hexagram 47, exhausting. this is a stark shift. the lake is empty, dried up. oppression, adversity, the feeling that there's nothing left. the judgment says: "OPPRESSION. Success. Perseverance. The great man brings about good fortune. No blame. When one has something to say, It is not believed."

this is the warning embedded in the transition. if you mishandle the limitations of hexagram 60 — if you impose them too harshly, or if you refuse to accept the natural ones — you end up here, in exhaustion. the lake dries up. the resources you were trying to manage are gone. but the judgment also says that even in this state, success is possible if you're strong within, if you remain true to yourself, if you don't let the external circumstances break your spirit.

the relating hexagram is teaching you what happens when limitation becomes deprivation. it's the endpoint of galling restriction, the place where the boundaries you set (or the ones imposed on you) have drained the system entirely. but it's also showing you that even in that exhausted state, there's a path forward — not through external action, but through internal resilience.

## synthesis

the eight of pentacles this morning was about steady, unglamorous work — the discipline of repetition, the humility of apprenticeship. that card is living in line four of hexagram 60: contented limitation. the craftsperson at the bench isn't fighting the limits of the material or the tools. they're working within them, finding success precisely because they understand the natural boundaries of the work. this is limitation that serves the craft, not limitation that chokes it.

the three-card spread shows the arc: two of wands (planning, vision, strategic choice), temperance (integration, patience, slow adjustment), three of swords reversed (healing, release, reconciliation). this is hexagram 60 in action. the two of wands is line one — knowing when not to go out the door, when to hold back and accumulate energy instead of spending it. temperance is the entire hexagram: finding the sustainable limit, the place where discipline supports growth without strangling it. and the three of swords reversed is what happens when you get it right — the pain starts to lift, the wound starts to heal, because you're not over-restricting yourself into exhaustion.

the tarot cards showed you the emotional and strategic path. the hexagram is showing you the structural principle underneath. you're being asked to examine your limits — not to abolish them, but to refine them. where are you holding too tightly? where are you imposing galling limitations on yourself, boundaries that feel punishing instead of supportive? because temperance, the card of moderation and balance, is saying exactly what line four says: find the natural limit, the one that flows, the one that works with you instead of against you.

the two of wands was about choice. hexagram 60 is about the limits that make choice possible. you can't choose everything; that's not freedom, that's paralysis. real power comes from knowing what you're willing to restrict, what you're willing to let go, so that what remains is sustainable, focused, effective. temperance is the process of figuring that out, of adjusting the proportions until they work.

and the three of swords reversed in your future? that's the payoff. when you stop over-restricting yourself, when you stop trying to control everything through sheer force of discipline, the pain starts to move. the grief lifts. not because you've ignored it, but because you've stopped strangling yourself with unsustainable boundaries. you've found the natural limit, the one that actually serves healing instead of prolonging the wound.

the relating hexagram — exhausting — is the warning. if you don't get this right, if you keep pushing the galling limitation, you'll end up drained. the lake will dry up. but you're not there yet. you're in the middle of the work, and the work is about finding the measure. the joint. the place where structure supports life.

the eight of pentacles is showing you how: through repetition, through patience, through the unglamorous work of refining your craft within the natural limits of the material. temperance is showing you the method: pouring water between cups, adjusting proportions, staying steady through the slow process. and hexagram 60 is showing you the principle: limitation works, but only if it's the right kind. only if it's contented, natural, sustainable. not galling. not strangling. not exhausting.

find the joint in the bamboo. that's where the strength is.
