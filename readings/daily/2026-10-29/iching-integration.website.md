**Eradicating (#21)**
*Eradicating, FIRE, THUNDER, THE CLINGING, THE AROUSING*

the image is a mouth with something caught between the teeth. not closed, not open -- a mouth that cannot shut because the obstruction is physically in the way. this is the hexagram of getting through something by force of the bite. the judgment is blunt about it: *"BITING THROUGH has success. It is favorable to let justice be administered."* success, yes, but on terms that include consequences, and the second sentence is the one people skip.

the image under the judgment is older and harder. thunder and lightning, and the kings of former times making the laws firm through clearly defined penalties. the point was never punishment as revenge. it was clarity of consequence. a law with no stated cost is a rumor, and rumors do not hold anything together.

thunder below, fire above. what rises from below is the arousing -- the jolt that starts the motion. what governs from above is the clinging -- clarity, light, the sun. fire alone would be too soft to get through anything. thunder alone would be too hard to be fair. together they make the just measure: hard enough to actually bite through, clear enough to know what you're biting and why.

five of the six lines are moving -- 1, 2, 4, 5, 6. this is a live transition, not a condition you observe from outside. line three holds still: old dried meat, striking something poisonous, slight humiliation. the stale grievance. it is still there. it is not the thing doing the moving.

each moving line carries its own instruction, and none of them repeat.

line one: feet fastened in the stocks, so the toes disappear, no blame. the first warning, applied early and lightly -- only the toes are in the stock. the correction lands while it is still small enough to stop the behavior instead of punishing a person. read it as timing: halt while halting is cheap. this line changing means your next move sets the size of everything after it.

line two: bites through tender meat, so the nose disappears, no blame. easy to tell right from wrong here, tender meat gives way -- but indignation blots out finer sensibility on the way. you go slightly too far because the anger feels righteous. no blame attaches to the penalty. something in your perception gets scoured away anyway.

line four: dried gristly meat, and metal arrows. mindful of difficulties, persevering, good fortune. this is the hard version of the work. the obstruction is powerful, the opposition is real, and the reward here is genuinely good fortune -- but it arrives with arrows and asks you to be hard as metal and straight as an arrow to do it. most of the moving energy in this hexagram is sitting on this line.

line five: dried lean meat, and yellow gold. perseveringly aware of danger. this is the one about fairness. yellow gold is the color of the middle -- impartial, no softness in it, no harshness in it. and then the part that gets skipped: stay conscious of the danger growing out of the responsibility you've taken on. the danger isn't only outside. the responsibility itself turns dangerous when it's held carelessly.

line six: neck fastened in the wooden cangue, so the ears disappear, misfortune. the failure state of the whole hexagram. the obstruction that won't be bitten through, the warning that stops being heard. ears disappearing means deaf to correction. this is year three, the thing still between the teeth, and nobody able to say so to you anymore. this line is changing too, and it is the one naming what you are avoiding.

which is where hexagram 29, Darkness, comes in. water doubled, the ravine. its name means repetition of danger -- not danger once, danger again, and the second time is worse because now you know its shape. the counsel is strange and good: be sincere, and whatever you do succeeds. sincerity is not the soft option here. it is the only thing that still penetrates a situation you have become used to. the question is whether you are the kind of thing that keeps its shape in the dark.

## the synthesis

the cards showed a burn in the past, a steady flame in the present, and an unopened cup ahead. the hexagram shows what that arrangement requires: bite through. the tarot tells you where the energy went wrong and where it's standing now. the i ching names the thing neither card will say for you -- the obstruction, the one that has been between your teeth so long you have started to talk around it.

they don't contradict each other. the tarot works at the scale of what you did and how you feel about it. the hexagram works at the scale of what keeps the pieces from meeting. a bite through is not a breakdown; it is the only way two things get close enough to touch. but line two warns the doing of it can cost you your finer sensibility if indignation drives, and line five says it has to be done impartially, with awareness of what the responsibility is doing to you while you hold it. neither asks you to be cold. they ask you to be precise.

so the practical shape is small. name the obstruction, once, plainly, in words that don't soften the edges you don't want softened. do it in the tone of the yellow gold rather than the tone of the arrow. then let the line three problem -- the old dried meat, the slightly humiliating stalemate -- stay where it is. it is not the thing that is moving. the moving lines are all about the correction you have been avoiding, and one correction applied while it is still small keeps you free of blame.

what you get for it is the future card, and the future card is not a guarantee. it is an ace. it is the outpouring of feeling and intuition and spiritual receptivity, which is exactly what the darkness hexagram says you will need: sincerity, the quality that keeps its shape when the situation gets familiar. the queen of wands is how you hold the present while you do this. the ace of cups is what the obstruction has been protecting this whole time.
