*heartbreak, sorrow, separation, truth, grief*

A clean truth can hurt without being cruel. The Three of Swords puts the ache in the room instead of asking you to decorate around it. Something may have ended, disappointed you, or revealed a distance you didn't want to see. You don't need to invent a silver lining before you know what you've lost.

Grief is not always dramatic. Sometimes it looks like reaching for the phone and remembering why you can't make that call. Sometimes it is a small, ordinary task made heavy because the person you wanted beside you isn't there. Give that reaction room. The pain is information about an attachment, not evidence that you were foolish to have one.

There is a difference between telling yourself the truth and using the truth as a weapon against yourself. “That relationship could not continue as it was” is different from “I ruin everything.” One names a circumstance; the other makes a sentence out of your whole life. Be careful which story you repeat when you're tired.

Today, let the practical response be as modest as the wound requires: cancel something optional, tell a trusted person what happened, or write down the sentence you've been avoiding. You don't have to disclose it to everyone. You do deserve a place where it doesn't have to be hidden.

This card does not promise an immediate reunion or a tidy ending. It asks for honesty and a little tenderness while the facts settle. A heart can be hurt and still be a heart: not an error to correct, not a machine that should have performed better.

You may want a precise date for when the ache will stop. Most hearts do not work on a delivery schedule. Measure care instead by whether you have somewhere to put the truth and someone, including yourself, willing to hear it.
