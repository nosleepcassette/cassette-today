**Great Harvest (#14)**
*great harvest, flame, heaven, the clinging*

the image of great harvest is fire in heaven above — imagine the sun, bright and full, high in a clear, vast sky. it’s not just light, it’s warmth, it’s energy, it’s the full force of cosmic benevolence shining down on you. this isn't just about receiving; it’s an instruction to mirror that clarity and power in your own life. you’re being asked to see what is good and what isn't, to lean into the positive, and to align your actions with a larger, benevolent current. this isn't passive acceptance. it’s active stewardship.

the judgment declares: *possession in great measure. supreme success.* this isn't a whisper of potential, it’s a statement of fact about your current reality. you have much. more than you might realize, perhaps. this isn't just about material wealth, but about influence, resources, capacity, and a deep well of inner fortitude. it’s a time when your efforts are paying off, when the seeds you’ve sown have come to fruition. the success is undeniable, but it comes with a certain weight — the weight of abundance, of having the means to do significant things. you’re not just holding a basket of fruit; you’re overseeing an entire orchard.

this hexagram names a time of undeniable fullness. you’ve arrived at a point where your resources, both tangible and intangible, are considerable. the posture it demands is one of conscious management and responsible leadership. it sees you in a position of power, not necessarily over others, but over your own circumstances and the bounty you’ve cultivated. the question isn't how to get more, but how to wisely handle what you already possess. you’ve put in the work, and now you’re living in the harvest. it’s a good problem to have.

the second line, **line 2**, speaks of *a big wagon for loading. one may undertake something. no blame.* you have the capacity. you have the tools, the infrastructure, the sheer volume to move mountains — or at least, to move your harvest. this isn't a time for small gestures or hesitant steps. your current resources are ample enough to support significant undertakings. you are equipped. there’s no judgment in stepping up to the plate, no fault in leveraging the abundance you’ve accumulated. load up that wagon.

the fifth line, **line 5**, offers a guide: *he whose truth is accessible, yet dignified, has good fortune.* this is about how you carry your success, how you wield your influence. you’re in a position where people are looking to you, where your actions and words carry weight. your integrity needs to be clear, understandable, and open for others to see. but this accessibility shouldn’t come at the cost of your self-respect or your inherent authority. be transparent, be honest, be approachable, but hold your ground. good fortune flows from this balance. your character is your compass.
