**Great Harvest (#14)**
_Fire, Heaven, The Clinging_

The image of Great Harvest describes fire in heaven above — a blaze that's being carefully managed, tended to, and harnessed. This isn't a wildfire, but a controlled burn, one that's being used to clear the way, to purify, and to bring light to the landscape. The instruction here is to take the same approach with the energies in your life: to manage them, to tend to them, and to use them for illumination and growth.

The judgment of Great Harvest says: "_POSSESSION IN GREAT MEASURE._" This isn't just about having a lot of something, but about having a lot of something that's truly yours — something that you've cultivated, that you've earned, and that you can truly call your own. It's about recognizing what you have, and using it to further your own growth, and the growth of those around you.

Great Harvest names a time of abundance, of expansion, and of growth. It demands a posture of openness, of receptivity, and of generosity. It sees that you have the power to create, to nurture, and to bring things to fruition — and it's urging you to use that power, to take action, and to make the most of the opportunities that are available to you. This hexagram is all about recognizing your own potential, and using it to create something of value, something that will last.

## the sky
**Sun in Leo (1st house)** — the sun is shining brightly in the area of your life that's all about self-expression, creativity, and personal growth.
**Mars in Virgo (2nd house)** — mars is pushing you to take action, to get things done, and to make progress in the area of your life that's all about work, health, and daily routines.
**Venus in Cancer (4th house)** — venus is bringing a sense of nurturing, of care, and of protection to the area of your life that's all about home, family, and emotional foundations.

## synthesis
The single card that set the morning's tone was the Six of Wands — a card of recognition, of acknowledgement, and of celebration. The three-card spread showed a psychological arc from the past (Six of Wands), to the present (Seven of Cups), to the future (Nine of Wands) — an arc that's all about moving from a place of external validation, to a place of inner questioning, to a place of self-protection and boundaries. The hexagram of Great Harvest shows the structural layer underneath both — a layer that's all about abundance, growth, and the responsible use of power.

The tarot and the I Ching are seeing the same thing from different angles — both are pointing to a time of growth, of expansion, and of increased responsibility. The tarot is showing the psychological and emotional nuances of this time, while the I Ching is showing the deeper, structural patterns that are at play. Where they confirm each other is in the importance of recognizing your own power, and using it to create something of value. Where the I Ching complicates the tarot is in its emphasis on the need for careful management, and responsible use of that power.

Practically, this means that you should be taking a posture of openness, receptivity, and generosity — but also of careful consideration, and responsible action. You should be recognizing your own potential, and using it to create something of value — but also being mindful of the potential risks, and challenges that come with growth, and expansion. You should be celebrating your successes, and acknowledging your achievements — but also being aware of the need for self-protection, and boundaries.
