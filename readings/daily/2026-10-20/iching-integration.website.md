*preponderance of the small, careful conduct, proportion, perseverance without overreach*

hexagram 62, little exceeding, puts a firm limit beside today's appetite for movement. its judgment says small things may be done; great things should not be done. that's a question of present conditions, not your ultimate potential. the cast asks you to notice the size of the action the situation can actually hold. a modest task done carefully may be appropriate where a sweeping move would create more exposure than progress.

the flying bird brings the message: don't strive upward; remain below. read that as a call to stay near the practical ground, not an order to make yourself lesser. check the details that keep a plan viable. answer the question that was asked. do the maintenance that prevents avoidable trouble. you don't have to announce a transformation when the useful thing is to tighten one loose screw.

the judgment's commentary also warns against modesty becoming empty form or subservience. that's an important boundary. caution isn't permission for other people to shrink your dignity. you can decline an oversized demand, keep your standards, and choose a smaller move without apologizing for existing. the scale of the task and the worth of the person doing it are unrelated measurements.

## the image — thunder on the mountain

the image describes thunder over mountain and asks for extra reverence in conduct, real grief in bereavement, and thrift in spending. these aren't three unrelated moral rules. they're examples of giving the actual obligation more weight than the performance around it. treat the person carefully. let loss matter without making a production of it. spend according to the resources you have, not the impression you want to create.

thunder can sound enormous in the mountains. in a tense situation, a small action can also carry farther than you expect. a careless sentence, an overlooked date, or a promise made to end an awkward conversation may have consequences out of proportion to the effort it took to produce. pay closer attention because the conditions are sensitive, not because you need to become frightened of every move.

## changing line 1 — don't launch before you're ready

“the bird meets with misfortune through flying.” the first changing line's commentary describes a bird leaving the nest before it is fledged. the problem isn't flight itself. it's a launch that exceeds the support currently available. an idea can be good and still need preparation. a conversation can be necessary and still benefit from knowing what you intend to ask.

check whether urgency is coming from the task or from your discomfort with waiting. if the groundwork isn't ready, don't use adrenaline as a replacement. collect the missing detail, rehearse the difficult opening, or ask for someone to review the plan. smaller preparation is not wasted movement. it can keep you from turning a correct purpose into an avoidable collision.

## changing line 4 — meet the moment without overshooting

“no blame. he meets him without passing by. going brings danger. one must be on guard. do not act. be constantly persevering.” the fourth changing line is unusually explicit about restraint. you've reached a point where continued pursuit isn't automatically helpful. meeting the actual situation is enough; don't rush past it into the outcome you wanted to force.

perseverance here belongs to your attention and standards, not to relentless outward motion. if you've asked the clear question, allow the answer to arrive. if you've reached the agreed limit, stop there. if a decision lacks necessary information, preserve the option instead of making a grand gesture to prove commitment. keeping the work safe can be a real task, even when nobody applauds a well-maintained pause.

only lines one and four change. together they distinguish premature departure from unnecessary continuation. before the launch, check readiness. once you've met the appropriate stopping point, don't turn persistence into overreach. restraint works differently at each stage, but both forms ask you to stay in contact with what is actually happening.

## possible movement — hexagram 36: Brilliance Injured

the relating hexagram is 36, brilliance injured. this is a possible condition or perspective, not a promise that harm is coming. its judgment calls for perseverance in adversity; its image places light beneath earth, veiled but still shining. you may need to protect your clarity instead of displaying every thought in a setting that cannot hold it well.

that doesn't mean assuming everyone is hostile. it means choosing the audience and timing for disclosure. some ideas need a trusted collaborator before a public argument. some limits need to be stated simply instead of defended with your entire inner history. discretion can protect what matters without requiring you to surrender it. keep the light available for the work, rather than spending it proving that you have one.
