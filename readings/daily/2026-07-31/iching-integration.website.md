**Replenishing (#48)** — Water over Wood · *wind, the abysmal*

The image is a well. Water over wood — the old kind of well, where a wooden bucket on a rope drops into underground spring. You don't move the well. You move yourself to it. The town can burn down and be rebuilt somewhere else, but the well stays where the water is. That's the first thing this hexagram wants you to understand: some things are not negotiable by relocation.

*The well. The town may be changed, but the well cannot be changed. It neither decreases nor increases. They come and go and draw from the well. If one gets down almost to the water and the rope does not go all the way, or the jug breaks, it brings misfortune.*

The judgment is almost insultingly plain. The well is fine. The problem is whether you're willing to lower the bucket all the way, and whether the container you're using can hold what you draw up. This is not a hexagram about finding a new source. It's about using the source that's already there — and using it properly.

This hexagram names a time when the resource is stable but the infrastructure around it is not. The water hasn't moved. You have. Or your tools have. Or your patience has. The well doesn't care. It will still be water. The question is whether you'll get any of it today.

**Line 2** — *At the well hole one shoots fishes.* You're using the well for something it wasn't made for. The water is there and you're trying to fish instead of drink. This is a warning about misdirected effort. You have access to what you need, but you're approaching it sideways, looking for a different kind of payoff. The well gives water. Stop trying to catch what lives in it.

**Line 5** — *In the well there is a clear, cold spring from which one can drink.* This is the payoff line. The water is clean. The bucket works. The rope reaches. Someone who knows what they're doing is drawing from it. This is not luck — it's alignment. The well has always been here. You just finally showed up with the right equipment and the patience to lower it all the way.

These two lines together tell a specific story: you've been fishing when you should have been drinking. Line 5 is the correction. Not a new well. Not a different source. The same one, used correctly.

## the relating hexagram

**Humbleness (#15)** — Earth over Mountain

The well leads to modesty. Not the performative kind, not self-deprecation. The real kind: knowing exactly what you have and exactly what you don't, and not pretending otherwise. The mountain sits under the earth here — it's solid but not visible. You don't need to announce your depth. You just need to be deep.

This is where the day is going. The well is the starting point. The destination is a quiet confidence that doesn't need to prove itself. When you've drawn from the real source, you don't have to tell anyone. It shows.

## the sky

**Mercury in Leo (9th house)** — You're thinking about what you believe, and you're thinking about it loudly. Mercury in Leo wants to make a statement. The 9th house wants it to mean something. Today your mind is not in the details — it's in the argument, the philosophy, the thing you'd defend at a dinner party. Useful for clarity. Less useful for the slow work of drawing water.

**Venus in Virgo (10th house)** — Careful love in public. Venus here wants things done right — professionally, visibly, with attention to how it looks and how it functions. This is not reckless affection. This is love that shows up on time with a spreadsheet. The 10th house wants it to matter to your reputation. Fine, as long as you remember that the well doesn't care about your reputation.

**Sun in Leo (8th house)** — The self in the deep end. Sun in Leo is naturally performative, but the 8th house is where performance stops working. This transit pulls your identity into the places you can't control — shared resources, intimacy, the stuff that doesn't photograph well. You want to shine. The 8th house says you can, but only if you're willing to be seen in the dark.

But the future card is Five of Swords. Which means the peace you're building now will be tested by someone who wants to win more than they want to be right. The King of Pentacles doesn't fight that fight. He doesn't need to. He has the well.

The hexagram is the structural layer underneath all of this. The tarot shows you the psychological arc — from reckoning to steadiness to potential conflict. The I Ching shows you what's actually true: the source hasn't changed. The well is still full. The only thing that matters is whether you're going to lower the bucket all the way, or keep fishing in the hole.

Mercury in Leo wants you to make a point. Venus in Virgo wants you to make it correctly. The Sun in the 8th house wants you to be seen in your depth. None of that matters if you don't have water.

The relating hexagram is Humbleness. Not because you're small. Because the well doesn't need to announce itself. It just needs to be used.

So use it. Lower the bucket. Don't break the jug. Drink.
