## hexagram 48: Replenishing

**Replenishing (#48), changing line 5, relating to Growing Upward (#46)**  
*the well, nourishment, shared resources, steady growth*

The image is a well. People come and go, and the town around it can change, but the need to draw water remains. Hexagram 48 turns your attention from the drama of a particular moment toward the sources that make ordinary life possible: food, rest, trustworthy company, skills, and the shared structures people depend on. The well matters because it can be used, not because it looks impressive from a distance.

The judgment includes a practical warning. If the rope does not reach the water, or the jug breaks before it gets home, being near a good source is not enough. There is a difference between knowing what would help and having a workable way to receive it. A supportive friend may exist, for example, but you still need a way to ask. Rest may be necessary, but your schedule has to contain somewhere for it to happen.

This is not a demand to become perfectly self-sufficient. The image speaks of people encouraging one another in their work. A shared resource needs care, and the person who draws from it can also help maintain it. Asking for assistance and contributing when you can are not opposing moral categories. They are part of the same exchange.

### changing line 5: clear, cold water

The fifth line describes a clear, cold spring in the well, available to drink. The emphasis is not on digging harder or proving how thirsty you are. It is on recognizing nourishment when it is present and allowing yourself to take it in. That may be more difficult than it sounds if you are accustomed to treating help as something you have to deserve through exhaustion.

Notice the quality of what is offered. Is it actually sustaining, or does it leave you more depleted? Clear water is a useful image for care that does not require you to obscure your needs. You can accept a small, reliable kindness without pretending it solves everything. Drinking from the well is not the same as claiming ownership of it.

### relating hexagram 46: Growing Upward

The movement toward Growing Upward suggests development through steady, grounded effort. A plant does not leap out of the soil by arguing with it. It grows through repeated contact with what supports it. This is a picture of gradual progress rather than a deadline for feeling better.

If you are tending a difficult situation, choose a pace you can return to. One conversation, one meal, one boundary, or one appointment may be enough for this stretch. The line between nourishment and growth is direct: it is easier to keep moving when the basic needs are not treated as optional.

## the synthesis

The cards showed loss, a shaken sense of belonging, anxious thought, and the wish to protect what remains. Hexagram 48 asks a different question beneath them: what is the dependable source, and can you actually reach it? It does not contradict the tarot or insist that grief should become optimism. It adds the practical work of replenishment to the emotional picture.

Look at the rope and the jug in your own situation. A resource might be available in theory but inaccessible because the process is too complicated, the hours do not fit, or asking feels frightening. Change the route where you can. Put a glass of water next to the bed, send a short message instead of composing the perfect explanation, or ask someone to sit with you while you make a call.

Also notice where you are the well for other people. Care is shared, but that does not make your supply limitless or make you responsible for every thirsty person. A boundary around your time can keep the exchange possible. The goal is not to close the well; it is to keep the rope intact and the water reachable.

The fifth line gives permission to drink. Growing Upward gives permission to take your time afterward. You do not have to turn today's nourishment into a recovery story by tonight. Let it support the next ordinary step, and leave the rest of the story room to unfold.
