## the card

**Five of Cups**
*loss, grief, regret, disappointment*

Something mattered, and it did not turn out the way you needed it to. The Five of Cups does not ask you to clean that up into a lesson before you have had a chance to feel it. Disappointment is information, not an inconvenience you have to hide so everyone else can stay comfortable.

This card looks directly at what has been spilled. That can be a relationship, a plan, a version of home, or simply the time and effort you put into something that could not hold it. You may catch yourself rehearsing the moment it went wrong, looking for the one sentence or decision that would have changed the outcome. Some reflection is useful. An endless trial where you are both defendant and judge is something else.

There are cups still standing in this image. They do not cancel the ones on the ground. They are a reminder that loss rarely takes every source of care with it, even when grief makes the remaining ones difficult to see. You do not have to feel grateful for what remains before you are allowed to mourn what is gone.

## what it means together

Give the disappointment a name today. Not the polished version: the actual thing you miss, wanted, or thought you would have by now. If a practical action belongs with that recognition, make it small enough to do without treating yourself like a problem to solve. Send the message asking for company. Eat something. Put the unfinished plan away until you can look at it without putting yourself on trial.

Then notice what is still available without demanding that it replace the loss. A person who listens, a routine that steadies you, or a quiet hour can be support without being a cure. You are allowed to receive that support while you are still sad.

The Five of Cups is honest about the part that cannot be restored exactly as it was. It is also honest about the part of your life that has not disappeared. Let both be true. There is no prize for pretending either one is smaller than it is.
